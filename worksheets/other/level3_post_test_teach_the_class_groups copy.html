Your Goal Today

Your group will create a short review game for one post-test topic. The game should help classmates practice, remember the rules, and feel more confident.

What Your Group Must Prepare

Make a game for the class. Your game should include clear directions, examples or question cards, correct answers, and a way for classmates to check or earn points.

Your Game Should Include
  • A game title.
  • Simple directions players can understand.
  • At least 8 question, sentence, or challenge cards.
  • An answer key so your group can check answers.
  • A way to score points, move turns, or choose a winner.
  • A role for every group member.
Ask For What You Need

If your group needs something, ask me. I can help you with materials, examples, directions, or ideas.

  • Notecards
  • Paper
  • Markers
  • Printed sentence cards
  • Printed word cards
  • A quick example or model
  • Game ideas if your group feels stuck

Game Tips
  • Explain the directions slowly and clearly.
  • Use examples from real life: work, school, health, family, or daily routines.
  • Make the game easy to start in under one minute.
  • Include answer choices, hints, or examples if the topic is hard.
  • It is okay to ask the teacher for help while you build or run the game.

Group 1: Comparative & Superlative Coaches

Create a game where classmates practice comparing two things and identifying the highest, lowest, best, worst, or most extreme in a group.

Mission

Your job is to create a review game that helps classmates choose and use comparatives and superlatives correctly.

What Your Game Should Practice
  • Comparatives compare two people or things.
  • Superlatives compare one person or thing to a whole group.
  • Short adjectives often use -er and -est.
  • Longer adjectives often use more and the most.
Post-Test Connection

Example: He is the fastest student in the class; he always finishes first.

Students also need this pattern for writing their own comparison sentence.

Grammar Guide
Use Pattern Example Clue
Comparativeadjective + -er + thanMy new job is better than my old job.Two things
Comparativemore + adjective + thanThis class is more helpful than the video.Two things
Superlativethe + adjective + -estHe is the fastest student in the class.One in a group
Superlativethe most + adjectiveShe is the most detail-oriented person on our team.One in a group
Common Mistakes To Explain
Incorrect: He is fastest student in the class.
Correct: He is the fastest student in the class.Use the before a superlative.
Incorrect: This job is more better than my old job.
Correct: This job is better than my old job.Do not use more with better.
Incorrect: She is more organized person in the group.
Correct: She is the most organized person in the group.Use the most when comparing one person to the group.
Possible Game Questions
  1. He is student in the class. (fast)
  2. This class is than the last class. (easy)
  3. Maria is person on our team. (detail-oriented)
  4. Write one comparative sentence about jobs, school, or health.
  5. Write one superlative sentence about the class.

Group 2: Parts of Speech Detectives

Create a game where classmates identify the job a word is doing inside a sentence.

Mission

Your job is to create a review game that helps classmates recognize nouns, verbs, adjectives, and adverbs in post-test sentences.

What Your Game Should Practice
  • Nouns name people, places, things, or ideas.
  • Verbs show action or being.
  • Adjectives describe nouns.
  • Adverbs describe verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs.
Post-Test Connection

Example: He finished the project quickly.

Finished = verb. Quickly = adverb because it tells how he finished.

Grammar Guide
Part of Speech Job Questions It Answers Examples
NounNames a person, place, thing, or ideaWho? What?student, project, health, transition
VerbShows action or beingWhat happens?finished, trained, studies, navigate
AdjectiveDescribes a nounWhat kind? Which one?beneficial, vocational, detail-oriented
AdverbDescribes a verb, adjective, or adverbHow? When? Where?quickly, carefully, already, never
Common Mistakes To Explain
Mistake: Thinking every -ed word is an adjective.
Example: He finished the project.Finished is a verb because it shows the action.
Mistake: Confusing adjective and adverb.
Example: He worked quickly.Quickly is an adverb because it describes the verb worked.
Mistake: Looking only at the word, not the sentence.
Strategy: Ask, "What job is this word doing here?"
Possible Game Questions
  1. In "He finished the project quickly," what part of speech is finished?
  2. In "He finished the project quickly," what part of speech is quickly?
  3. In "Daily exercise is beneficial," what part of speech is beneficial?
  4. In "The apartment needs maintenance," what part of speech is maintenance?
  5. Write one sentence and underline one noun, one verb, and one adjective.

Group 3: Connector Coaches

Create a game where classmates choose connectors for reasons, results, contrasts, time, and conditions.

Mission

Your job is to create a review game that helps classmates choose the correct connector and notice what relationship it shows between ideas.

Connectors To Use In Your Game
  • because
  • so
  • although
  • if
  • as soon as
  • when
Post-Test Connection

I forgot to drink water all day, so I felt very thirsty.

She was thirsty, so she drank water.

If she takes classes, she will get a better job.

Connector Guide
Connector Shows Pattern Example
becausereasonresult because reasonI felt tired because I slept late.
soresultreason, so resultI slept late, so I felt tired.
althoughcontrastalthough surprising idea, main ideaAlthough it was hard, I finished.
ifconditionIf + present, will + verbIf I pass the test, I will celebrate.
as soon astimeone thing happens immediately after anotherCall me as soon as you arrive.
whentimeone event connected to anotherWhen class ends, I will go home.
Common Mistakes To Explain
Incorrect: I forgot to drink water because I felt thirsty.
Better: I forgot to drink water, so I felt thirsty.The forgotten water is the reason. Feeling thirsty is the result.
Incorrect: If I will get the job, I will celebrate.
Correct: If I get the job, I will celebrate.Use present tense in the if-clause.
Incorrect: She was thirsty because she drank water.
Better: She was thirsty, so she drank water.Drinking water is the result, not the reason.
Possible Game Questions
  1. I forgot to drink water all day, I felt very thirsty.
  2. She was thirsty, she drank water.
  3. she takes classes, she will get a better job.
  4. Fix it: If I will get the job, I will celebrate.
  5. Write one sentence with because and one sentence with so.

Group 4: Perfect Tense Experts

Create a game where classmates practice present perfect, perfect tenses, and perfect continuous tenses from the Level 3 post-test review.

Mission

Your job is to create a review game that helps classmates use have/has + V3 and have/has been + verb-ing correctly.

What Your Game Should Practice
  • present perfect
  • present perfect continuous
  • perfect tense word order
  • irregular V3 forms
  • for vs. since
  • how long questions
Important Clues

Present perfect: before, already, yet, never, many times, twice, three times.

For: a length of time, like for three years.

Since: a starting point, like since January or since she moved.

Grammar Guide
Tense Formula Example Use
Present perfecthave/has + V3She has eaten sushi before.Life experience or past action connected to now.
Present perfect negativehave/has not + V3I have not written the report yet.Something not completed before now.
Present perfect continuoushave/has been + verb-ingShe has been driving since January.Action started in the past and continues now.
How long questionHow long + have/has + subject + been + verb-ing?How long have you been speaking English?Asks about duration.
Common Mistakes To Explain
Incorrect: He has went to the career center twice.
Correct: He has gone to the career center twice.Use the V3 after has/have.
Incorrect: I have lived here since three years.
Correct: I have lived here for three years.Use for with a length of time.
Incorrect: She has study English since January.
Correct: She has been studying English since January.For an action continuing now, use has been + verb-ing.
Incorrect: She already has sent her resume.
Correct: She has already sent her resume.Already usually goes between has/have and the V3.
Possible Game Questions
  1. She (eat) sushi before.
  2. Derek (forget) his password many times.
  3. I (not write) the report yet.
  4. She has been studying English she moved to Boston.
  5. They (run) a small business for five years.
Quick Verb Relay

Ask the class for the V3: eat -> eaten, forget -> forgotten, see -> seen, be -> been, take -> taken, go -> gone, write -> written.

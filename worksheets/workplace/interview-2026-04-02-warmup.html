Reading

Artemis II: A New Mission to the Moon
By Class News  |  April 2, 2026

Yesterday, Artemis II launched successfully. Many people had been watching the mission for months before the launch. Scientists were preparing for years, and the astronauts had trained every day. When the rocket lifted off, people around the world were cheering and recording the moment. By the time the spacecraft reached space, it had already made history.

Today, on April 2, people are talking about the mission everywhere. News reporters are explaining what happened, and scientists are studying the data. The crew is traveling around the Moon and is sending updates back to Earth. The astronauts are checking the life support systems, running tests on the spacecraft, and collecting data about deep space. They are also communicating with Mission Control every few hours to report what they are observing.

This mission is especially important because no one has traveled to the Moon in over 50 years. The last time the United States sent astronauts to the Moon was in 1972, during the Apollo program. At that time, twelve people walked on the Moon's surface. After Apollo ended, humans stopped going to the Moon. For decades, scientists had been dreaming of returning. Now, Artemis II is making that dream real again.

In the future, this mission will help humans return to the Moon. Scientists will use the information they have collected to plan new missions. By the time the next mission launches, engineers will have improved the technology even more. One day, people may be living and working on the Moon. Artemis II is showing us what will be possible.

1) What happened yesterday?
2) What are the astronauts doing right now?
3) How will this mission help in the future?
4) How did people feel when the rocket lifted off? How do you know?

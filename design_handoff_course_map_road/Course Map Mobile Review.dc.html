Why it works for students
1
It always opens on "You are here". The big dot (in the unit's color) and one big button answer the main question: what do I do now?
2
One road, no folders. Units are signs along the road, not boxes you open. Weeks never move, so September is always above October.
3
"Back to this week" appears when you need it. As soon as you scroll away, a button shows up, like the "re-center" button in a map app.
4
The top bar tells you where you're looking. The month, unit, week, and dates update as you scroll. The whole course is one line: tap any piece to jump there.
5
Real dates, not just numbers. "Oct 5–9" matches the classroom calendar and the student's own phone. "Week 5" alone means nothing outside the app.
6
Easy-to-read type. Atkinson Hyperlegible (already in your worksheets folder) makes letters like I/l/1 and O/0 easier to tell apart. The base size is 15–16px.
What's gone: the week dropdown, unit cards inside unit cards, "Previous / Upcoming" groups, "Week details", and lists that reorder themselves. Past weeks that aren't finished just show "2 of 3 done", with no warnings, so the focus stays on this week.
7
Built around your two cycles. The top bar is one swipeable strip for the whole year, with a line between Cycle 1 and Cycle 2. It slides along as you scroll the map. Swipe it back to October and tap a week to jump there. In April, all of Cycle 1 is one dark "21 of 21 weeks done" banner, and February and March fold into short signs. Tap any of them to open it in place.
8
Future weeks are visible but locked. Students see what's coming and when it opens ("Opens Monday, Apr 19"), so the road still leads somewhere. They can't open it early. Later units show only their name and start date. To compare, switch Future weeks to "Open early" in Tweaks.
Try it: switch Time of year to April in Tweaks to see the map late in the year. scroll the left phone up to September and watch the top bar change and the "Back to this week" button appear. Then tap a future week to look inside it.

2b Student test script · about 20 minutes
Can students find their way on the map?
3 students, one at a time, on their own phone. About 5 minutes each. Run it once on today's map, then again after the fixes, and compare.
Before you start
• Start them on the Home tab, not the map.
• Say: "I'm testing the app, not you. If something is confusing, that's the app's fault."
• Ask them to think out loud, in any language.
• Don't help. If they're stuck for 30 seconds, say "What would you try?", then move on.
Tasks (read them out loud)
1
"Open the course map. What week is the class on?"
Watch: do they find the Map tab? Do they read the header or guess?
2
"What unit is that? What month?"
Watch: can they answer without opening anything?
3
"Show me the next thing you need to do." (Point, don't tap.)
Watch: do they point at the right activity?
4
"Find Week 1."
Watch: what do they tap first? Do they notice when the screen changes?
5
"Now go back to this week."
This is the most important task. Watch for scrolling around or going back to Home.
6
"Where would you find extra games for this week? Do you have to do them?"
Watch: do they know optional practice is optional?

Where students could get lost
1
The unit name disappears when you open a unit. The month, unit name, and colored badge are replaced by "This week". The only place it says "Unit 1" is inside the closed "Week details" section.
2
The main navigation looks like a label. "Week 2 ⌄" is a hidden dropdown that lists every week in the course in one long system menu. There's no "previous week" or "next week" button.
3
The heading changes its wording. It says "This week", "Viewing", or "Coming up" depending on the week. The biggest text on the screen keeps changing meaning in the same spot.
4
Weeks change order when you tap one. The open week jumps to the top and the others regroup into "Previous" and "Upcoming". After a tap, the week you chose is no longer where it was.
5
Weeks and activities use the same shape. Week numbers sit in round outlines, just like the "not done" activity circles above them.
6
It's unclear where Unit 1 ends. Unit 1 is a card that contains weeks, and the other units are separate cards below it. Tapping Unit 2 closes Unit 1 and moves the whole page.
7
Nothing shows your place in the course. "1/6 finished" only covers this week. Nothing says "Week 2 of 8" or "September".
8
Optional practice looks like required work. It uses the same row size and type as the required activities.
What already works: the activity rows. They have big tap targets, clear done / next / to-do icons, format chips, and a single "Continue" label. All three redesigns keep them as they are.

Level 3 Post-Test Prep

Teach the Class Review Game

Create a short review game that helps classmates practice one post-test grammar skill.

Big Goal

Make a game, not a long lesson. Classmates should practice, talk, guess, fix mistakes, and explain why. Keep grammar clear, useful, and a little fun.

Your Game Should Include

  • A game title
  • Simple directions
  • At least 8 questions, cards, or challenges
  • An answer key
  • A way to check answers or give points
  • A job for every person in your group

Your Game Should Help Students

  • Notice clues in sentences
  • Choose the correct verb form or adjective form
  • Fix common mistakes
  • Explain why an answer is correct
  • Feel more ready for the post-test

Game Ideas You Can Choose From

Card Sort

Students sort words, sentences, or mistakes into groups.

Fix the Mistake

Students find the error, correct it, and explain the rule.

Sentence Race

Teams write correct sentences using the grammar target.

Who Am I?

Students guess the tense, adjective form, connector, or helping verb from clues.

Timeline Challenge

Students place events in order and choose the correct tense.

Mini Quiz Show

Students answer questions in teams and explain their answers.

Before You Start

Do This

  • Use examples from real life.
  • Make the directions easy.
  • Practice saying the directions before you teach.
  • Make sure everyone in your group speaks.

Do Not Do This

  • Do not copy a long grammar chart and read it.
  • Do not make only one person teach.
  • Do not make the game too complicated.
  • Do not forget the answer key.

Group 1

Time Clue Detectives

Mission: Help classmates use time words to choose the correct verb tense. Time words are clues for when something happened and which verb form fits.

What You Need to Teach Through Your Game

  • Past, present, and future time clues
  • Which tense usually goes with each clue
  • How to notice clue words in a sentence
  • Common mistakes with time words

Helpful Time Clues

yesterday last week every day right now since for tomorrow by 8:00

Quick Grammar Guide

Time Clue Usually Use Example
yesterday, last year, in 2010, when I was a child Past simple I moved to Boston in 2010.
every day, always, usually, sometimes Present simple She takes the bus every day.
right now, currently, at the moment Present continuous They are studying right now.
since, for, already, yet, ever, never Present perfect or present perfect continuous He has lived here for five years.
tomorrow, next week, soon, later Future with will or be going to We are going to meet tomorrow.
by 8:00, by the time, before a future moment Future perfect She will have finished by 8:00.

Common Mistakes to Practice

✗ I have seen her yesterday.
✓ I saw her yesterday.
Yesterday = finished past time, so use past simple.
✗ I am go to school every day.
✓ I go to school every day.
Every day = habit, so use present simple.
✗ She will finishes by 5:00.
✓ She will have finished by 5:00.
By 5:00 focuses on completion before a future time.

Game Ideas for This Group

  • Signal Word Sort: Sort time clues into past, present, and future.
  • Tense Clue Challenge: Pick a clue word and make a correct sentence.
  • Timeline Race: Put events on a timeline and choose the tense.
  • Who Am I? Read a sentence. Classmates guess the tense and clue.

Perfect Tense Pros

Mission: Help classmates understand perfect tenses. Perfect tenses connect two times: before now, before another past action, or before a future time.

What You Need to Teach Through Your Game

  • Perfect tenses use have, has, had, or will have
  • The main verb usually uses V3, the past participle
  • Perfect continuous uses been + -ing
  • Perfect tenses often show completion, experience, or duration

Key Pattern

TensePattern
Present perfecthave / has + V3
Past perfecthad + V3
Future perfectwill have + V3
Perfect continuoushave / had / will have + been + -ing

Quick Grammar Guide

Tense Example Key Idea
Present perfect I have eaten. Experience or action before now
Past perfect I had eaten before she arrived. One past action happened before another past action
Future perfect I will have eaten by 8:00. Action will be finished before a future time
Present perfect continuous I have been studying for two hours. Started before now and is still happening or still important

Common Mistakes to Practice

✗ I have went to the store.
✓ I have gone to the store.
Perfect tense needs V3, not simple past.
✗ I was living here since 2020.
✓ I have been living here since 2020.
Since often needs present perfect continuous for duration.
✗ I have been eat dinner.
✓ I have been eating dinner.
After have been, use the -ing form.

Game Ideas for This Group

  • V3 Match: Match base verbs with past participles.
  • Before What? Students decide which action happened first.
  • Perfect or Simple? Choose between “I ate” and “I have eaten.”
  • Fix the Timeline: Put two events in order and write the sentence.

Group 3

Helping Verb Heroes

Mission: Help classmates remember auxiliary verbs (be, have, do, will) in questions, negatives, continuous, perfect, and future sentences.

What You Need to Teach Through Your Game

  • When to use be, have, do, and will
  • How helping verbs change in questions and negatives
  • Why “do/does/did” appears in simple tense questions
  • How to fix common auxiliary verb mistakes

Auxiliary Verb Cheat Sheet

AuxiliaryUsed For
becontinuous tenses
haveperfect tenses
dosimple tense questions and negatives
willfuture

Quick Grammar Guide

Auxiliary Example Important Reminder
be: am, is, are, was, were She is working. Use with -ing for continuous tenses.
have: have, has, had They have finished. Use with V3 for perfect tenses.
do: do, does, did Did you go? After did, use base verb.
will I will call you. After will, use base verb. No “to.”

Common Mistakes to Practice

✗ He not working now.
✓ He is not working now.
Continuous tense needs be + -ing.
✗ Did you went to the party?
✓ Did you go to the party?
After did, use the base form.
✗ I will to go tomorrow.
✓ I will go tomorrow.
After will, use the base form without “to.”

Game Ideas for This Group

  • Missing Helper: Students add the missing auxiliary verb.
  • Question Machine: Turn statements into questions.
  • Auxiliary Sort: Sort sentences by be, have, do, or will.
  • Fix the Helper: Correct sentences with wrong helping verbs.

Comparison Captains

Mission: Help classmates compare correctly. Teach when to use comparatives (bigger than) and superlatives (the biggest).

What You Need to Teach Through Your Game

  • Comparatives compare two things
  • Superlatives compare one thing to a whole group
  • Short adjectives usually use -er and -est
  • Long adjectives usually use more and the most
  • Some adjectives are irregular, like good, better, best

Helpful Comparison Words

bigger than smaller than more expensive than less crowded than the biggest the most important better than the best

Quick Grammar Guide

Meaning Form Example
Compare two things adjective + -er + than Boston is colder than Atlanta.
Compare two things with a long adjective more + adjective + than English is more difficult than math.
Compare one thing in a group the + adjective + -est This is the coldest day of the year.
Compare one thing in a group with a long adjective the most + adjective This is the most expensive apartment.
Irregular adjectives good → better → best This restaurant is better than that one.

Common Mistakes to Practice

✗ She is more taller than me.
✓ She is taller than me.
Do not use more + -er together.
✗ This is best restaurant.
✓ This is the best restaurant.
Superlatives usually need the.
✗ My phone is expensive than yours.
✓ My phone is more expensive than yours.
Long adjectives usually use more.

Game Ideas for This Group

  • Compare the Room: Compare real objects, people, or places.
  • Two or Group? Comparative or superlative?
  • Fix the Comparison: Correct “more bigger” and similar errors.
  • Ranking Game: Rank items and make superlative sentences.

Optional Group 5

Sentence Connector Coaches

Mission: Help classmates connect ideas clearly. Connectors show the relationship between two ideas, like reason, result, contrast, time, or condition.

What You Need to Teach Through Your Game

  • How connectors change the meaning of a sentence
  • Which connectors show reason, result, contrast, time, or condition
  • How to combine two short sentences
  • How punctuation changes with some connectors

Connector Types

because so but although when before after if

Quick Grammar Guide

Relationship Connectors Example
Reason because I stayed home because I was sick.
Result so I was sick, so I stayed home.
Contrast but, although Although I was tired, I went to class.
Time when, before, after I ate dinner after I finished work.
Condition if If it rains, I will take the bus.

Common Mistakes to Practice

✗ I was sick, because I stayed home.
✓ I stayed home because I was sick.
Because gives the reason.
✗ Although I was tired, but I went to class.
✓ Although I was tired, I went to class.
Do not use although and but together in the same clause.
✗ If it will rain, I will stay home.
✓ If it rains, I will stay home.
In real future conditions, use present simple after if.

Game Ideas for This Group

  • Connector Match: Match two ideas with the best connector.
  • Reason or Result? Decide if the sentence needs because or so.
  • Sentence Surgery: Fix connector and punctuation mistakes.
  • Build a Story: Use connectors to join events in order.

  5. I will to call you later.

  7. She is more taller than me.
  8. This is best restaurant.
  9. Although I was tired, but I studied.
  10. If it will rain, I will stay home.

Choose-the-Best-Answer Cards

  1. Yesterday, I ____ to the store.
    go / went / have gone
  2. She ____ English every day.
    studies / is studying / studied
  3. Right now, they ____ lunch.
    eat / ate / are eating
  4. I ____ here since 2021.
    live / lived / have lived
  5. By 8:00, we ____ dinner.
    will finish / will have finished / finished
  6. ____ you like coffee?
    Do / Are / Have
  7. He did not ____ yesterday.
    worked / work / working
  8. My apartment is ____ than my old apartment.
    big / bigger / biggest
  9. This is ____ expensive restaurant in the neighborhood.
    more / most / the most
  10. I was tired, ____ I went to bed.
    because / so / although

Explain-Your-Answer Prompts

  • What clue word helped you?
  • What tense do we need?
  • What is the helping verb?
  • Are we comparing two things or one thing in a group?
  • Do we need -er, more, -est, or the most?
  • Is this a habit, happening now, finished past time, or duration?
  • Which action happened first?
  • What relationship does the connector show?

Easy Scoring Options

  • 1 point for the correct answer
  • 1 bonus point for explaining why
  • Team gets 30 seconds to discuss
  • Another team can steal if the answer is incorrect
  • No points, just move forward on a board game path

Student Reminder

A good review game does not need to be fancy. Students should practice the skill, check answers, and explain why an answer is correct.

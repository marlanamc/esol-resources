Part B: Phrases

8 min

Use modals: must/have to = obligation, should = advice, can/may = permission. 💬 Partner: say your sentence before writing.

1) Must / have to
  • You must take a break every 4 hrs. You have to wear safety equipment.

Supervisor says no break for 6 hours. What rule do you mention?

Answer:
2) Should
  • You should document problems. You should talk to your supervisor.

Coworker isn’t paid for overtime. What do you tell them?

Answer:
3) Can I…? / May I…?
  • Can I leave early? (informal) May I take a longer break? (formal)

You have a doctor’s appointment. Ask your boss (one question).

Answer:
4) Can / are allowed to
  • You can take breaks. You are allowed to refuse unsafe work.

Someone says workers can’t refuse dangerous tasks. Correct them.

Answer:
5) Don’t have to / must not
  • You don’t have to work overtime. You must not work without training.

Friend thinks they must work every weekend. One sentence with don’t have to, one with must not.

Answer:

Tip: must = strong rule, have to = requirement, should = advice, can/may = permission. Use a Part A word when you can ✓

Part C: Past Perfect Timeline

8 min

Past perfect connects two past events. The earlier event uses had + past participle (or had been + -ing for past perfect continuous). The later event uses simple past. Both events are before NOW.

Remember: Past perfect = emphasize result. Past perfect continuous = emphasize duration.

Example: When I started at Harborside, I had already been practicing English for three years.

# ESOL Level 3 Post-Test Review: Teach the Class Activity

## Teacher Setup

Students work in 3 groups. Each group becomes the "expert group" for one part of the Level 3 post-test review. Their job is to teach the class, give examples, explain common mistakes, and lead a short practice activity.

Suggested timing:

- 10 minutes: groups prepare
- 5-7 minutes per group: group teaches
- 2-3 minutes per group: class practice or questions
- 5 minutes: whole-class reflection

Each group should prepare:

- 3 key ideas to teach
- 3 example sentences
- 2 common mistakes and corrections
- 1 short practice activity for the class

---

## Group 1: Vocabulary Navigators

### Mission

You are the Vocabulary Navigators. Your job is to help your classmates understand and use important post-test vocabulary in real sentences.

You will focus on these words:

- vocational
- transition
- beneficial
- navigate
- maintenance
- detail-oriented
- set up

### What You'll Teach

Teach your classmates:

- What each word means in simple English
- How to use each word in a sentence
- Which words are nouns, verbs, adjectives, or phrasal verbs
- How these words connect to work, school, health, housing, and daily life

### Word Guide

| Word | Simple Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| vocational | related to job training | She is taking a vocational class to learn medical billing. |
| transition | a big change from one situation to another | Moving to a new city was a big transition. |
| beneficial | helpful; good for you | Daily exercise is beneficial for your health. |
| navigate | to find your way through something | He used a map to navigate the neighborhood. |
| maintenance | work that keeps something in good condition | The building needs regular maintenance. |
| detail-oriented | careful with small details | Accountants must be detail-oriented. |
| set up | arrange or prepare | My assistant needs to set up more interviews. |

### Common Mistakes to Explain

1. Incorrect: Exercise is benefit for your health.
   Correct: Exercise is beneficial for your health.
   Explain: Benefit is a noun or verb. Beneficial is an adjective.

2. Incorrect: She is taking a vocation class.
   Correct: She is taking a vocational class.
   Explain: Use vocational before a noun like class, school, program, or training.

3. Incorrect: I need set up an appointment.
   Correct: I need to set up an appointment.
   Explain: After need, use to + verb.

### How You Might Teach It

- Vocabulary Match: Match each word to its meaning.
- Sentence Fix: Show incorrect sentences and ask the class to fix them.
- Real-Life Categories: Put each word under work, school, health, housing, or daily life.
- Act It Out: Act out set up, navigate, maintenance, and transition.

### Class Practice

Ask classmates to complete these sentences:

1. Learning English is beneficial because __________.
2. A detail-oriented person is good at __________.
3. I had to navigate __________.
4. A big transition in my life was __________.
5. I need to set up __________.

## Group 2: Grammar Pattern Coaches

### Mission

You are the Grammar Pattern Coaches. Your job is to teach the grammar patterns that appear in the multiple-choice and writing parts of the post-test review.

You will focus on:

- because vs. so
- first conditional
- superlatives
- used to
- gerunds and infinitives
- parts of speech

### What You'll Teach

Teach your classmates:

- How to choose the correct grammar pattern
- What clue words to look for
- How to explain why an answer is correct
- How to avoid common grammar traps

### Grammar Guide

| Topic | Pattern | Example | Key Idea |
|---|---|---|---|
| because | result because reason | I felt thirsty because I forgot to drink water. | Because gives the reason. |
| so | reason, so result | I forgot to drink water, so I felt thirsty. | So gives the result. |
| first conditional | If + present, will + verb | If she takes classes, she will get a better job. | Real future possibility. |
| superlative | the + -est / the most + adjective | He is the fastest student. | Compares one person or thing to a group. |
| used to | used to + base verb | I used to go to bed late. | Past habit, not true now. |
| infinitive | decide + to + verb | I decided to jog. | Some verbs are followed by to + verb. |
| parts of speech | verb, adverb, noun, adjective | He finished the project quickly. | Finished = verb; quickly = adverb. |

### Common Mistakes to Explain

1. Incorrect: If I will get the job, I will celebrate.
   Correct: If I get the job, I will celebrate.
   Explain: In the if part, use present tense, not will.

2. Incorrect: I am used to go to bed late when I was in college.
   Correct: I used to go to bed late when I was in college.
   Explain: Used to + base verb means a past habit.

3. Incorrect: I decided jogging early in the morning.
   Correct: I decided to jog early in the morning.
   Explain: Decide is followed by to + verb.

4. Incorrect: He is faster student in the class.
   Correct: He is the fastest student in the class.
   Explain: Use the with superlatives.

### How You Might Teach It

- Pattern Cards: Give classmates sentence cards and ask them to name the grammar pattern.
- Because or So Race: Students choose because or so and explain the reason.
- Fix the Sentence: Show common mistakes and let the class correct them.
- Parts of Speech Detective: Underline one word and ask, "What job is this word doing?"

### Class Practice

Ask classmates to fix or complete these:

1. If I will pass the test, I will celebrate.
2. She was tired, __________ she went to bed early.
3. I decided __________ study more English.
4. Maria is __________ organized person in our group.
5. In this sentence, what part of speech is quickly? "She answered quickly."

## Group 3: Perfect Tense Experts

### Mission

You are the Perfect Tense Experts. Your job is to help classmates understand present perfect and present perfect continuous, especially with irregular verbs, for, since, already, never, before, and yet.

You will focus on:

- present perfect
- present perfect continuous
- irregular past participles
- for vs. since
- word order with already and never
- questions with how long

### What You'll Teach

Teach your classmates:

- How to form present perfect
- How to form present perfect continuous
- When to use for and since
- How to use irregular participles like eaten, forgotten, seen, been, taken, gone, written
- How to fix common mistakes

### Grammar Guide

| Tense | Formula | Example | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Present perfect | have/has + past participle | She has eaten sushi before. | Life experience or past action connected to now. |
| Present perfect negative | have/has not + past participle | I have not written the report yet. | Something not completed before now. |
| Present perfect continuous | have/has been + verb-ing | She has been driving since January. | Action started in the past and continues now. |
| How long question | How long + have/has + subject + been + verb-ing? | How long have you been speaking English? | Asks about duration. |

### Important Clues

Use present perfect with:

- before
- already
- yet
- never
- many times
- twice
- three times

Use for with a length of time:

- for three years
- for five months
- for two hours

Use since with a starting point:

- since January
- since 2020
- since she moved to Boston

### Common Mistakes to Explain

1. Incorrect: He has went to the career center twice.
   Correct: He has gone to the career center twice.
   Explain: Use the past participle after has/have.

2. Incorrect: I have lived here since three years.
   Correct: I have lived here for three years.
   Explain: Use for with a length of time.

3. Incorrect: She has study English since January.
   Correct: She has been studying English since January.
   Explain: For an action continuing now, use has been + verb-ing.

4. Incorrect: She already has sent her resume.
   Correct: She has already sent her resume.
   Explain: Already usually goes between has/have and the past participle.

### How You Might Teach It

- Verb Form Relay: Say the base verb and classmates give the past participle: eat -> eaten, forget -> forgotten, see -> seen.
- For or Since Sort: Sort time expressions into for and since.
- Perfect Tense Formula Board: Write have/has + V3 and have/has been + -ing, then build sentences.
- Unscramble Challenge: Give mixed-up words and ask classmates to make a correct sentence.

### Class Practice

Ask classmates to complete these:

1. She __________ (eat) sushi before.
2. Derek __________ (forget) his password many times.
3. I __________ (not write) the report yet.
4. She has been studying English __________ she moved to Boston.
5. They __________ (run) a small business for five years.

## Whole-Class Wrap-Up

After all groups teach, each student writes:

1. One vocabulary word I can use now: __________
2. One grammar pattern I understand better: __________
3. One mistake I need to avoid: __________
4. One question I still have: __________

Optional exit ticket:

Write one sentence using one vocabulary word and one post-test grammar pattern.

Example: If I take a vocational class, I will make a beneficial transition to a new career.

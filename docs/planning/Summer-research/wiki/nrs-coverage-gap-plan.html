NRS 3–4 Coverage Gap Plan

Audit of NRS Level 3–4 topics and skills missing from the current class and app, with unit mapping and a summer build checklist.

NRS Level 3–4 Coverage Gap Plan

Purpose

This document compares the current class, app, and worksheet coverage against NRS ESL Level 3 (High Beginning) and Level 4 (Low Intermediate) expectations. It identifies topics and skill types that are underrepresented so they can be addressed in next year's curriculum and app path.

Naming note: The community class is called "ESOL Level 3," but in official NRS terms the class spans roughly NRS high Level 2 through Level 5, with a goal that students reach at least NRS Level 4 by the end of the year. This plan uses official NRS numbering when referring to standards.

Decision filter: Every gap below should become a real task students can do — not a checklist item with no class connection.

What Is Already Strong

The current program aligns well with NRS 3–4 in these areas:

AreaCurrent coverage
HousingLandlord calls, repairs, lease language, tenant problems
Workforce / careerInterviews, applications, rights, advocacy, phrasal verbs, reported speech
HealthSymptoms, clinic visits, pharmacy, insurance cards, medicine labels, MyChart
Community lifeResources, participation, daily routines, warm-ups
Grammar depthOften above NRS 3–4 minimum (future perfect, conditionals, passive voice, reported speech, etc.)

The gaps below are not failures. They are underdeveloped strands that NRS treats as core life/work skills or cross-cutting modalities.

Topic Gaps To Address

1. Digital literacy and technology

NRS expectation: Level 3 includes following instructions to log into a computer system; Level 4 expects gathering information from print and digital sources.

Split into two layers:

LayerWhenContent
Layer 1: App survivalSeptember (Unit 1)Chromebook, Chrome, myesolclass.com, login, password reset, find Next Up / This Week's Path, app scavenger hunt
Layer 2: Adult digital lifeOctober+Scams, forms, email safety, health/job portals, online applications

Current status: MyChart and insurance portal content exist for Layer 2, but Layer 1 is not yet a taught September boot camp. Password reset often requires teacher manual help (login uses username; forgot-password uses email).

Layer 1 target outcomes (September):

  • Open Chromebook → Chrome → myesolclass.com
  • Log in with username and password
  • Reset password (self-service or teacher temp password)
  • Find what we are working on today in the app

Layer 2 target outcomes (October+):

  • Spot a suspicious email or text
  • Complete a simple online form
  • Log into a health or job portal

Where it fits: Layer 1 → September App Onboarding Plan. Layer 2 → October (Daily Life in the Community) or cross-unit micro-tasks.

2. Phone English (systematic)

NRS expectation: Short phone conversations for immediate needs — calling in sick, emergencies, insurance, clarifying information.

Current status: Some speaking warmups (landlord, banking) exist, but phone calls are not a repeatable format across units or in the app path.

Target outcomes:

  • Leave a clear voicemail
  • Ask someone to repeat or speak slowly
  • Call a clinic, landlord, or employer with a script frame
  • Confirm information at the end of a call

Where it fits: Every unit — health (insurance), housing (repairs), work (absence), school (absence/message)

3. Pay stubs, taxes, and wage math

NRS expectation: Workplace functional skills include schedules, wages, and forms; Illinois math integration expects adults to read bills and compare amounts.

Current status: Mentioned in improvement plan and app roadmap; banking warmup exists but pay stub reading is not a built lesson.

Target outcomes:

  • Read gross vs. net pay
  • Identify hours, rate, overtime, and common deductions
  • Ask HR or a supervisor about a pay question
  • Connect wages to budgeting

Where it fits: February (Workforce) or March (Career Awareness)

4. Consumer smarts (beyond vocabulary)

NRS expectation: Shopping, comparing prices, reading bills/fees, and handling everyday consumer decisions.

Current status: December Consumer Smarts unit is planned; shopping/finance vocab exists; case studies touch tariffs/scams in print only.

Target outcomes:

  • Compare unit prices
  • Read a bill or fee notice
  • Understand returns/refunds language
  • Question a misleading offer

Where it fits: December (Consumer Smarts)

5. Parent, school, and childcare communication

NRS expectation: Level 3 examples include "Do your children go to school here?" and school registration forms.

Current status: Scattered references in vocab and planning; no dedicated taught unit.

Target outcomes:

  • Read a school notice or report card
  • Write an absence note or message to a teacher
  • Prepare for a parent–teacher conference
  • Ask about childcare or after-school programs

Where it fits: September (Getting to Know You) or October (Daily Life in the Community)

6. Civic participation and voting (substantive)

NRS expectation: Level 4 moves toward claims, reasons, and evaluating information — relevant for voting and public meetings.

Current status: November Community Participation unit and Level 1 civic vocab exist, but voting, ballots, and contacting officials are thin compared with workplace/health depth.

Target outcomes:

  • Understand voter registration basics
  • Read a ballot question or public notice
  • Contact a local office or elected official
  • Participate in a community meeting with question frames

Where it fits: November (Community Participation)

7. Legal literacy beyond the workplace

NRS / adult-life expectation: Know which agency handles which problem; document issues in writing.

Current status: Workplace rights are strong; broader legal tasks (tenant law basics, small claims language, immigration pathways) live on the resource website but not in the class spine.

Target outcomes:

  • Match a problem to the right agency
  • Write a clear complaint summary
  • Understand when to seek legal aid (without giving legal advice)
  • Use resource-site legal categories in class

Where it fits: March (Career Awareness / advocacy) and cross-link to resource website

8. Transportation as a full unit strand

NRS expectation: Shopping, transportation, and health are core "immediate needs" contexts; bus schedules, fares, and directions appear repeatedly.

Current status: One undated transportation warmup; scattered examples elsewhere.

Target outcomes:

  • Read a bus or train schedule
  • Buy a ticket or pay a fare
  • Give and follow directions using a map
  • Report a transit problem

Where it fits: October (Daily Life in the Community)

9. STEM / science informational literacy

NRS guiding principles: Instruction should include informational texts in content areas including STEM, with increasing complexity across levels.

Current status: Health/nutrition and current-events case studies exist; little on reading science explainers, charts, risk, or cause/effect in health/science contexts.

Target outcomes:

  • Read a short science or health news explainer
  • Interpret a simple chart or graph
  • Distinguish observation from opinion in a short text

Where it fits: April (Health) and May (Holistic Wellness); current-events case studies

10. Math embedded in ESOL

Illinois standards: ESL Levels 3–4 pair with ABE math — fractions for recipes, graphs for transportation, money comparisons.

Current status: Roadmap acknowledges this; app is mostly language-first.

Target outcomes:

  • Compare prices and percentages in consumer tasks
  • Read a chart or schedule with numbers
  • Use math language in speaking ("It costs 20% more," "Half the class takes the bus")

Where it fits: December, February, October — one numeric task per unit where natural

Priority Build Order (Summer And Fall)

If time is limited, address gaps in this order:

  1. September app boot camp — Chromebook, myesolclass.com, login, password, scavenger hunt, find today's work
  2. Work and money — pay stub, hours, deductions (February/March)
  3. Phone-call routines — reusable template across health/housing/work (all year)
  4. Digital life Layer 2 — scams, forms, portals (October, after boot camp)
  5. School and family week — notices, absences, parent meetings (September/October)
  6. Consumer smarts app tasks — compare prices, read a bill (December)
  7. Case-study / research template — read → claim → reason → discuss (November+)
  8. Relative clauses micro-module — small grammar gap for NRS 3

    Questions To Revisit

    • Which gaps matter most for this specific student group (parents, workers, caregivers, new arrivals)?
    • Which gaps can be one-week topics vs. recurring micro-tasks across the year?
    • Which resource website categories should become required class connections (not just links)?
    • How much embedded math is realistic without turning ESOL into a math class?
    • Should civic/voting content stay nonpartisan and local (Boston/East Boston context)?

    Working Thesis

    NRS 3–4 is not only a grammar map. It is a set of real adult tasks in housing, work, health, community, money, school, and civic life — plus the skills to read, speak, write, call, research, and question information in English.

    Next year should keep the strong grammar and topic work already built, and deliberately fill the thinner strands: digital life, phone English, wages/taxes, school/family, civic depth, transportation, consumer protection, legal navigation, STEM/data literacy, and math-in-context.

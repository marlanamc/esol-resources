-ed Endings Hear it. Clap it. Say it.

Keep this page!

We write -ed, but we say 3 different sounds. Ask 2 questions:

Question 1: Does the verb end in a T or D sound? YES → add an extra clap: /id/
want → want-id   need → need-id   decide → decide-id
Question 2: NO? Then no extra clap. Whisper sound (only air)? → /t/
Everything else → /d/
Not sure? Don't worry: just don't add a clap!
The #1 mistake: an extra clap.   walk-ed → walkt    cook-ed → cookt    play-ed → playd

Teaching order (why this works)

  • Claps first, voicing later (or never). The extra syllable is the mistake listeners notice. /t/ vs /d/ mostly happens on its own once students stop adding a clap.
  • Same 3 colors all year: blue /t/, orange /d/, purple /id/. Use them on the board, the chart, and the app.
  • Last sound, not last letter: decide, like, hate end in a silent e; fix ends in ks; laugh ends in f.
  • Be honest about listening: before a consonant ("walked to") the /t/ almost disappears. Teach students to listen for time words.

Quick, low-pressure correction

  • Recast, don't stop: Student: "I walk-ed." You: "Oh, you walkt to work? Nice!"
  • Show, don't say: hold up 1 finger (no extra clap) or tap the color on the chart.
  • One word, one time: "Walkt. Say it with me." Then move on.
  • Praise the noticing: "You heard the extra clap. Great ears."

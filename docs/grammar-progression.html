Level 3 ESOL · FY27 · September 2026 to June 2027

Level 3 Grammar Progression

How grammar builds across the year, one required guide a week. The unit topics set the context; the grammar is sequenced so each new point stands on something students already practiced.

How the year is built

One required guide a week

Each week has a single required grammar guide. Anything else is an optional extra, marked Challenge, for students who want more.

Strands come back

Present perfect, conditionals, modals and gerunds each return several times with weeks in between, so students forget a little and then rebuild it.

Review weeks close each stretch

W1 and W3 in September, W15 before the winter break, and W34 to W36 at the end of the year. Reviews cover only what has been taught; anything ahead is labeled as a preview.

Quizzes match the guides

Each weekly quiz pulls its grammar questions from that week's required guide, so students are never quizzed on something they haven't seen.

Month by month

What students can do as the year goes on

SeptemberW1–W3
Getting to Know You

Welcome back with four tenses on one bus ride, then the jobs words do: nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs.

By the end: talk about routines, what's happening now, and a past story; name the job of each word in a sentence.

OctoberW4–W7
Daily Life in the Community

The five verb forms (V1, V1-s, V-ing, V2, V3), wh- questions, routines and how often, imperatives, and the zero and first conditional timed with the election.

By the end: ask questions that get real answers, give directions, and say what happens if: "If you go early, the line is short."

NovemberW8–W10
Community Participation

Can, should and must; the present perfect for life experience; adjective + preposition + -ing.

By the end: give advice and rules, ask "Have you ever...?", and say what they're good at and interested in.

DecemberW11–W15
Consumer Smarts

How long + for and since, comparatives and superlatives, countable and uncountable nouns, will and going to in the news, then a review of all six tenses learned so far.

By the end: compare prices, talk about quantities, read an economy headline, and make predictions and plans.

JanuaryW16–W19
Housing & Renting

Direct and indirect questions; have to, don't have to and can't; infinitives after need, want and hope; past continuous with when and while.

By the end: ask a landlord polite questions, explain lease rules, and tell the story of a housing problem.

FebruaryW20–W23
Workforce Preparation

Past perfect, workplace rules with must, have to and should, the second conditional, and phrasal verbs.

By the end: put past events in order on a resume, explain workplace rules, and imagine "What would you do if...?"

MarchW24–W27
Career Awareness

Just, already and yet in a job search; the present perfect continuous; gerund or infinitive; the passive voice.

By the end: talk about where a job application stands, describe ongoing work, and read job ads and pay stubs.

AprilW28–W31
Health & Healthcare

Should, ought to and had better; stop, try and remember with -ing or to; reported speech; the third conditional.

By the end: follow medicine instructions, report what the doctor said, and talk about past regrets.

MayW32–W35
Holistic Wellness

Used to; be used to and get used to; all four conditionals together; gerunds and infinitives together.

By the end: compare life before and now, describe adjusting to change, and choose the right conditional or verb pattern.

JuneW36
Year in Review & Next Steps

Every tense from the year on one timeline, will vs. going to, and a labeled sneak peek at the Level 4 future tenses.

By the end: tell a story across past, present and future, and talk about plans for next year.

Things to watch
  • April is the densest month. W29, W30 and W31 are three of the hardest points of the year back to back. W30 has catch-up time built in; check those quiz scores.
  • W32 used to → W33 be used to is a classic confusing pair. W33's "Don't Mix Them Up" page targets it; check the W33 quiz.
  • Modals take five guides (W8, W17, W21, W28, plus W7's optional suggestions). If W21 is ever freed up, relative clauses (who, that, which) are the biggest gap and suit the workforce unit.
Keeping it in sync

When a guide changes

  • Rebuild the quizzes with npm run quizzes:build. CI fails if a guide changes without it (check:generated). Students see the new questions after quizzes:sync -- --apply.
  • The course map syncs on deploy. Edits to src/lib/course-map-data.ts reach students when the PR deploys.
  • A new guide needs a released activity row, or the finished-guide checkmark won't save. Run scripts/course-map/ensure-guide-activities.ts; it previews first and is safe to re-run.
  • Production writes need ALLOW_PROD_DB_MUTATION=yes CONFIRM_DB_HOST=db.prisma.io and node --env-file=.env --import tsx ....

# Parts of Speech: September–October first pass

The five phases organize revisitable ideas, not mastery gates. Completing a short lesson does not certify mastery. Check-ins help the teacher choose what to revisit. No score unlocks a subsequent lesson.

| Timing | Focus | Small transfer task |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Week 3 / late September | Nouns and verbs | Say or write a sentence about your day; find a noun and verb. |
| September or early October, as needed | Quick foundation check-in: basic pronouns and a/an/the | Use a pronoun and article in one sentence. |
| Week 4 | Adjectives and articles | Describe one thing at home. |
| Week 5 | Subjects and verbs, with an explicit word-type / sentence-role bridge | Say or write a sentence about someone; find its subject and verb. |
| Week 6 | Simple objects and descriptions after be | Choose an action sentence or a description. |
| Week 7 | Common adverbs of manner and frequency, with adjective review | Say how or how often you do something. |

Weeks 4–7 have two examples, six focus questions, and two familiar questions from the preceding weekly lesson. Week 3 establishes the initial reference set. The transfer task is ungraded, spoken or written on paper, and not recorded by the app. It does not block saving or completion.

The phase menu retains core lessons, non-gating check-ins, and optional depth. Detailed determiners, indirect objects, object complements, dense verb-form terminology, comparative/placement details, and long lists of preposition partnerships are not required for the first pass. Phase 5 teaches specific word partnerships rather than inferring a pattern solely from a part-of-speech label.

September is a starting target, not a deadline: carry unfinished foundation work into October when needed. October is an introductory pass through Phases 2–3, not a requirement to finish the original library's rounds.

## Progress and release

Revised weekly question sets have separate lesson IDs. Existing noun/verb, adjective/article, optional-lesson, and library records remain intact. Each weekly map tile checks its own lesson record. The course-map seed, already part of the normal build, applies the new links; no production database writes were made during local verification.

Before whole-class assignment, try Week 3 with two or three students. Ask them to explain one label and produce one sentence; use their responses to adjust pace rather than treating completion or accuracy alone as readiness.

## Visual cues

Category colors and icons repeat across worked examples, answer labels, feedback, and annotated transfer examples. Nouns use blue, verbs coral, adjectives plum, and articles gold; sentence roles have their own labeled cues. Text labels always accompany the color key. Unanswered targets stay neutral so color does not reveal the answer. The completion panel denotes finishing the lesson, not mastery; the ungraded sentence task has a separate warm panel.

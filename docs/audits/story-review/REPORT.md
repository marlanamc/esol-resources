# Grammar guide story review

Reviewed 2026-10-03. Each guide's packet (`npm run audit:stories`) was read against [RUBRIC.md](RUBRIC.md). The verdicts are editorial judgment, not test results.

**40 guides: 21 Revise · 18 Polish · 1 Keep**

> **Status (2026-10-03, later):** continuity fixes were applied to all 18 guides that scored 1 on "Makes sense". Contradictions between story, exercises and quiz, and timelines, names and jobs, were fixed without rewriting the stories. The scores below are from *before* those fixes. Still open: story rewrites for guides that are dull or off theme (Interest/Theme scores of 1), and whether to keep teaching "said to me that" as an error in Reported Speech. Packets are regenerated, so re-score with the rubric when you revisit a guide.

## Patterns across the guides

1. **Exercises and quizzes contradict the story (the most common problem).** The story says Amara, the exercise says Bruno. A hotel job turns into a warehouse job. Someone "quit last month" while they're still at work in the scene. Eighteen guides scored 1 on "Makes sense", almost always because of this. When you rewrite a story, re-read every exercise and quiz item against it.
2. **The same names keep changing roles.** Jennifer is a restaurant supervisor, then a warehouse supervisor, then a coworker. Rosa, Mark, Kevin and Carlos turn up with different jobs and families. Either keep a stable cast sheet (Rosa is always a housekeeper with a daughter), or use different names.
3. **Timelines don't add up.** Dates, days and shift times clash: housekeeping since 2021 "for three years", a Friday 2 PM request for Friday off, an 8:15 AM shift end followed by a 3 PM shift end.
4. **The week's theme is missing or pasted on.** Some guides ignore their theme: Your Week in English (directions/digital safety), What Are You Good At? (public meetings), Zero + First Conditional (voting). Others paste a heritage-month note into a caption that has nothing to do with the dialogue.
5. **Routines with nothing at stake.** The guides that score well (I Need to Find a Place, It Was Happening When, Third Conditional) have a deadline, money or a problem. The weak ones are people describing their week.
6. **Teacher's-pet lines.** Characters name the grammar ("That's an adjective"), coach it out loud, or give a public-service announcement.
7. **Photos that don't match captions.** These were flagged in about 8 guides.

### Welcome Back: Simple & Continuous Review (`welcome-back-tenses-review`), Week 1
Scores: Clear 2 · Sense 2 · Cringe 3 · Theme 3 · Interest 2 → **Polish**
One-line story: Rosa catches up with David on the first week back, and her sister is covering the kids, but the shoe story is told twice by two different people.
Problems:
- Section 4: an unnamed "Student" tells nearly the same shoe story Rosa told in section 1 ("One shoe was still under the couch" vs. "I found his other shoe under the sofa"). A tired reader will think it's Rosa, but it's a son, not a daughter.
- Mini quiz item 3: "We couldn't find her shoes. Then my daughter ___ under the sofa and found them." This mixes the two versions. In section 1 the daughter asks Rosa where the shoes are. She doesn't find them.
- Section 2: "the minute we leave the house" and "My daughter eats it on the bus" mean the daughter rides with Rosa. But section 1 has Rosa going to the bus alone after work while her sister has the kids.
- Section 3 photo: the caption is the sister cooking at home ("Where's the rice?"), but the photo is an empty classroom.
- Section 1: the dialogue is heavy on exclamation points ("Almost!", "Totally!", "MY notebook!"). It's mild.
Best fix: Make section 4 Rosa's own story from that morning, told to the teacher. Put her son, or the same daughter, at the bus stop with mismatched shoes, so section 1's "shoe under the couch" pays off as the missing shoe. Then make the quiz item match.

### Parts of Speech Guide (`parts-of-speech`), Week 2 (also Week 3; Week 16)
Scores: Clear 2 · Sense 2 · Cringe 1 · Theme 2 · Interest 2 → **Revise**
One-line story: Amara registers for an English class at the community center and gets help from James, Carlos and Dilnoza. Then two new people analyze a flyer.
Problems:
- Section 5: Yemi and Linh have never been introduced ("After signing up, Yemi and Linh..."). Their whole dialogue is grammar analysis: "English and September are both nouns, right?" / "'Free' describes 'classes.' That's an adjective." / "So 'every Tuesday' does an adverb's job... Got it!" These are characters announcing the grammar.
- Section 2 exercise: "Carlos wants to practice his Haitian Creole." This comes from nowhere and doesn't fit the character.
- Mini quiz item 4: "Amara tells the class about her sister's work schedule." Amara has no sister in the story. Dilnoza is the one who lives with her sister.
- Section 4 photo: the caption is the community center desk, but the photo is "a person in gloves writing on a clipboard during a cleanup event."
- Section 4: the backdrop is volunteer-hours paperwork ("How often can you volunteer, Carlos?"). The spec warns against this community-center default. Section 2 opens a small problem that never pays off: Amara can't find the Tuesday class on the form.
- Week 16 (Housing) fit is thin. Only "a small space for four people" touches it.
Best fix: Rewrite section 5 as Amara and Dilnoza reading the flyer to solve the section 2 problem: which room, what time, what to bring ("It says the large room. Bring a small notebook. I only have a big one."). Leave the word-type labels to the exercises. Make the quiz item about Dilnoza's sister, and swap the section 4 photo.

### Past Simple + Past Continuous: Telling the Story (`past-simple-past-continuous`), Week 4
Scores: Clear 2 · Sense 1 · Cringe 3 · Theme 2 · Interest 2 → **Revise**
One-line story: Fred misses the 111 bus and is late, and Elena leaves a double shift when her son falls at school. These are two stories that don't connect.
Problems:
- Section 1 intro: "His neighbor Michael had the same problem last week." Michael never appears.
- Section 2: "And you were carrying those groceries too, right?" Groceries at 7:40 AM on the way to class or work make no sense, and Amara wasn't there, so how does she know?
- Sections 1–2 contradict each other. The bus "left one minute early" and Fred "ran to the corner but it was already gone," but then "I was listening to music. I didn't hear it coming." Section 3 adds another version: "The bus left while he was crossing the street."
- Section 2 scene: "Blue Line train." It's not clear if Fred is telling Amara this on the train or later.
- Section 4: Elena's story has a real moment but stops flat. "I got there in ten minutes" → "Ten minutes? I'm glad you got there so quickly." The question about who covered her shift is never answered.
- Mini quiz: "While Amara ___ at the bus stop, Fred called her" and "Rosa was at work when the doctor called" are both events that aren't in the story. Item 1 also asks students to name the tense ("Which tense is this?").
- Theme (learn how to learn, study routine): the theme only appears as Fred being late, and even that is vague.
Best fix: Pick one version of the bus story. Fred is buying coffee (was buying) when the 111 pulls away early (left), so he is late for class. Drop Michael and the groceries. End Elena's scene with a payoff: "Did someone cover for you?" "My manager did. First time ever."

### Verb Forms + Your Study Toolkit (`verb-forms-overview`), Week 4
Scores: Clear 3 · Sense 2 · Cringe 2 · Theme 2 · Interest 3 → **Polish**
One-line story: Carlos grabs the wrong lunch bag at the restaurant and finds Mark's engagement ring, and he gets it back to Mark at the café just in time.
Problems:
- Section 4: "Later that night, Carlos checks the class group chat." Sarah and Mark aren't classmates, so they wouldn't be in a class chat.
- Section 2/3 logic: Mark was "looking everywhere in the kitchen" for his bag, but he had Carlos's bag ("I ate your sandwich at lunch"). He would know there was a swap and who to ask.

### Questions That Get Real Answers (`questions-real-answers`), Week 5
Scores: Clear 2 · Sense 2 · Cringe 2 · Theme 3 · Interest 2 → **Polish**
One-line story: In five short, unconnected scenes, people ask questions about a fake bank email, a lost download, backing up photos, a nosy flashlight app and a fake delivery text.
Problems:
- Structure: there are five casts in a single-topic guide, so it reads like a set of digital-safety tip cards and not a story.
- Section 1: Sarah reads like a public-service announcement. "I don't click anything." / "Good idea. If you're still worried, call the number on your bank card." Section 5 does the same: "Good. How often do you get these texts?"
- Section 3 photo: "Two women talking in a modern office," but the scene is Jean and a tech helper.
- Section 1 photo: students greeting before class doesn't match Rosa asking about a strange email.
- Section 1 exercise repeats the same item twice: "___ is the sender of this email?"

### Getting There: Directions + Imperatives (`getting-there-directions`), Week 6
Scores: Clear 2 · Sense 1 · Cringe 3 · Theme 3 · Interest 1 → **Revise**
One-line story: Separate people get directions to the community center, the clinic and the library. Nothing is at stake.
Problems:
- Section 3 contradiction: Linh says "It's next to the pharmacy," then "between the pharmacy and the bakery, on the corner." Something between two buildings isn't on the corner. Then the exercise says "The clinic is _____ the pharmacy. (opposite side of the street)," which contradicts "next to the pharmacy."
- Section 1 intro: "Hannah from the community center told her to walk up Meridian Street." Hannah never appears, and Amara then calls Jean for the same directions.
- Section 4: the steps are out of order. "The library is on the right... Finally, cross at the crosswalk and walk in front of the big blue doors." He has already arrived, so the last step makes no sense. The extra "flower market on Chelsea Street" text is leisure filler that doesn't connect.
- Section 2 notice typo: "Don't turn right at the front door. that leads to the storage room."
- Interest: no one has a reason to hurry or a problem to solve. They just get directions.
Best fix: Give Amara a deadline. She has a 7:15 clinic appointment and gets off the Blue Line at 7:00. Jean texts directions, the clinic door has the "Don't use the side entrance after 8 PM" notice, and Linh points her to the right building ("across from the laundromat, next to the pharmacy"). Make the section 3 exercise match.

### Your Week in English (`your-week-in-english`), Week 6
Scores: Clear 2 · Sense 1 · Cringe 2 · Theme 1 · Interest 1 → **Revise**
One-line story: Five unconnected people each describe their weekly routines.
Problems:
- Section 1 exercise contradicts the dialogue. Fatima says "I'm sometimes late," but the exercise says "Fatima is always on time for class." Rosa says "I always take the 111," but the exercise asks for "Rosa ___ takes the 111... about 80% of the time" (usually).
- Section 3: the scene is "Linh checks her phone calendar on the bus," but she is talking to a classmate and says "I come here on Tuesdays and Thursdays." "Here" isn't the bus.
- Section 4: the coworker's lines are textbook-y. "Have you gone to your English class?" / "I have already gone twice... And I have already done all my homework." / "You work hard!"
- Section 5: Valentina accepts twice ("Saturday would be good" ... "Saturday works for us. I'll check with the kids"). One line just repeats the other.
- Theme: the week is Getting Around: Directions + Digital Safety, and only the bus mention touches it. The interest problem is exactly the rubric's "characters just describe their routines."
- Mini quiz: "Rosa describes her morning commute," but Rosa's bus is to evening class.
Best fix: Build it around one person's week that gets disrupted. For example, Rosa usually takes the 111, but this week it's detoured, so she checks the MBTA app every morning and has already been late twice. That uses frequency adverbs, present simple and "already," and it fits the getting-around theme. Fix the Fatima and Rosa exercise contradictions.

### Let's Make a Suggestion (`lets-make-a-suggestion`), Week 7
Scores: Clear 2 · Sense 2 · Cringe 3 · Theme 2 · Interest 3 → **Polish**
One-line story: Tenants with no heat for two days plan what to do and decide to call the city. A restaurant shift-coverage scene interrupts in the middle.
Problems:
- Section 2: Kevin is "the building manager's assistant," but he tells tenants to report his boss to Inspectional Services ("They can force him to fix it"). That isn't plausible for the landlord's staff.
- Section 1 exercise: "We could take photos of the leak tonight." There is no leak. The problem is the heat.
- Section 4 caption: "Two coworkers review options together," but Gloria and Jean are neighbors.
- Section 3: the restaurant scene (Amara, Diego, Brian) breaks up the heat story. Then section 4 says "Diego gives a polite option to his manager Brian," but the scene never says Brian is a manager.
- Mini quiz: "Rosa and her neighbor agree on a plan." Rosa isn't in the story.
- Theme (Our Voice, Our Vote; contacting an official): calling the city fits loosely, but voting and elected officials never appear.
Best fix: Make Kevin a staff person at the city councilor's office whom Marta calls, which hits "contact an official." Change "leak" to "the thermostat," and the caption to "neighbors." Optionally, replace section 3 with the tenants asking "Should we email the councilor too?"

### Zero + First Conditional: If This, Then That (`zero-first-conditional`), Week 7
Scores: Clear 2 · Sense 1 · Cringe 2 · Theme 1 · Interest 2 → **Revise**
One-line story: In five separate scenes, people at work plan around shifts, bills and childcare.
Problems:
- Section 4: the timeline is impossible. "If I work this Saturday night, I will pay the rent on Friday." Saturday's pay can't cover the Friday before it.
- Section 2: Ana asks "Will you get paid before the bill is due next Friday?" Carlos never answers, and the plan has the same timing problem.
- Section 4: "if someone calls in sick, the whole shift stays. That's policy too" is a confusing rule. "That's the policy. It's been that way for years" sounds like it's explaining the grammar.
- Section 3: "When the bonus comes in" has no setup. The exercise gloss is garbled: "Jennifer means: 'I will be available IF my days off are not changed.'"
- Mini quiz: "Jennifer plans her rent around her work schedule." That isn't in the story.
- Theme: the week is Our Voice, Our Vote (civic, the November elections), and the story ignores it completely.
Best fix: Reset it around voting while working. Zero conditional for the rules: "If you're in line by 8 PM, you can vote." "If you work Tuesday, you vote early." First conditional for Carlos's plan: "If I vote early Saturday, I'll still make my shift." Fix Marco to "I'll have the rent by the 1st."

### Can, Should, Must (`can-should-must`), Week 8
Scores: Clear 2 · Sense 2 · Cringe 2 · Theme 2 · Interest 2 → **Polish**
One-line story: Four unconnected phone-safety scenes: a phone setup, a flyer, a workshop lecture and a scam text.
Problems:
- Structure: there are four casts, and sections 2–3 are people relaying safety tips ("This flyer says you should..." / "Exactly." / "Everyone, listen carefully."). It's close to a lesson in disguise. Only section 4, Ana's scam text, has a real problem.
- Section 4: "It says I won a prize... It says it's from my bank." A bank prize is an odd scam setup.
- Section 1 exercise: "You ___ (can / can't) share your password with anyone." This uses can't for a rule, which blurs the ability/rule line the guide teaches.
- Theme: Phone English shows up, but Family Connection is missing.

The library tech helper says what she must not do (share PINs or codes). That connects the safety lesson to the family theme.


### Have You Ever...? (`have-you-ever`), Week 9
Scores: Clear 2 · Sense 2 · Cringe 2 · Theme 2 · Interest 2 → **Polish**
One-line story: Four separate chats about past experiences: two Haitian cousins plan a family dinner, two classmates talk about Somali food, and two volunteers answer orientation questions.
Problems:
- §1 setup: Jean is "in Montreal", but he says "I've seen that place" about a restaurant in Maverick Square and then plans a family dinner there. It isn't clear where the dinner is or who is travelling.
- §1: One Haitian cousin asks another, "Have you ever tried Haitian food in Boston?" A Haitian woman would not normally be asked if she has ever tried Haitian food.
- §1 intro: "Emily from work called earlier, but tonight Claudette wants to catch up with family." This is a pasted-in American name that does nothing in the story. The photo is "reading a text message", but the scene is a phone call.
- §3/§4: There is stock volunteer cheer: "But I am excited to learn." and "so I would love to help." Both scenes are interview Q&A with no problem in them.

- §2/§4 exercises: "Did you ever try Guatemalan food?" is marked wrong, but American speakers say "Did you ever...?" all the time. A key that says it's wrong will confuse students when they hear it at work.

Give Minh in §4 a reason to say yes, such as "My neighbor needs someone to translate at her housing meeting", in place of "I would love to help".

### Just, Already, Yet (`just-already-yet`), Week 9
Scores: Clear 3 · Sense 1 · Cringe 2 · Theme 2 · Interest 2 → **Revise**
One-line story: Five errand scenes (community center desk, pharmacy, a bill, a to-do list, food pantry sign-up) about what's done and what isn't.
Problems:
- §5 exercise "What does it mean?": "Bruno has just found out about the program." In the dialogue, Amara says "I have just found out about it." Bruno registered last week. The exercise gives the line to the wrong person.
- §4 exercises (twice): "I have ___ paid the rent. I did it on Monday." The answer key wants "already". Present perfect with a finished time ("on Monday") is the exact error that have-you-ever teaches students to avoid in the same week.
- §1: A community center front desk asks, "are you here to pick up your EBT card?" EBT cards are mailed by DTA, so students know nobody picks them up at a community center. Also, "Mark from her building called about the food pantry too" uses "too" without anything before it, and Mark never appears again.
- §4 Rosa: "The kids had Monday off from school. I have already taken them to the clinic." The two sentences don't connect. "I have just called the school about my daughter. Still on my mind." reads like notes, not speech.
- §1 staff line: "Oh great, you have already taken care of that." This is stiff and exists to model the grammar.
- Theme: Week 9 is Helping + Volunteering, but this guide is about receiving help (pantry, EBT), not helping. That's acceptable as background only.
- Quiz item 4: "Nadine registered for the ESL program before today's class." The story never mentions this. Her open task was the food pantry.
- Positive: §3 (Linh's son, electric bill "due tomorrow") and §5 ("This month's deadline has just passed") have real stakes. Keep both.
Best fix: Change the §5 exercise item to "Amara has just found out about the program." Rewrite the §4 rent item as "I have ___ paid the rent. The receipt is on the fridge." Change §1 to "are you here to sign up for the food pantry?", where Nadine says "I have already signed up. I came in last week" and "I haven't gotten my pickup day yet".

### What Are You Good At? (`what-are-you-good-at`), Week 10
Scores: Clear 3 · Sense 2 · Cringe 2 · Theme 1 · Interest 2 → **Revise**
One-line story: Four job-hunting scenes (restaurant break, after a housecleaning interview, a job counselor, a skills form) using adjective + preposition + -ing.
Problems:

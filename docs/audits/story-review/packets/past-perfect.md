## 1. She Had Already Done the Work

> 🖼 **Scene:** East Boston. A warehouse off Chelsea Street. Wednesday morning.  
> _Photo shows: Worker in a warehouse walking between shelves of inventory boxes._

- **Gloria:** I'm nervous. I've never applied to a warehouse before.
- **Receptionist:** Don't worry. The manager said you already have good experience.
- **Gloria:** That’s true. Before I applied, I had worked in housekeeping for two years. I just hope that’s enough.

Past Perfect = something that finished before another past moment. Use it to show which action happened first when telling a story about the past.

The first action (working in housekeeping) uses had + past participle. The second action (applied) uses Past Simple. You almost always need both verbs to make the sequence clear.

- When the manager called her, she had already sent her application. _(options: She sent her application / The manager called her)_

**Exercise: Complete the sentence**
- By the time she got the interview, she ___ (work) at the hotel for two years.
- When the manager came out, Gloria ___ (already arrive) at the front desk.

## 2. had + V3: Putting It in Order

> 🖼 **Scene:** Break room, 10 AM. Gloria meets James, a forklift operator.  
> _Photo shows: Two coworkers talking at a table in a workplace break room._

- **James:** So what did you put on your application?
- **Gloria:** I wrote that I had cleaned hotel rooms and had handled supply orders. Never drove a forklift, though.
- **James:** Same. I hadn't driven one either before they trained me here.

Positive: subject + had + past participle Negative: subject + hadn't + past participle Every person uses had. There is no "hads" or "has had."

## 3. When, Before, After, By the Time

> 🖼 **Scene:** HR office. Jennifer, the hiring manager, interviews Gloria.  
> _Photo shows: A woman sitting across a desk from an interviewer in a bright office._

- **Jennifer:** Had you ever done inventory work before you applied here?
- **Gloria:** Yes. After I had finished my shift at the hotel, I helped count stock.
- **Jennifer:** And by the time you left that job, had you trained anyone?
- **Gloria:** I had trained two new cleaners before I left.

Four words help you show which action came first. Learn them and you can use Past Perfect naturally.

When she arrived, the manager had already called her references. When + Past Simple shows the second action. Past Perfect shows what finished first.

She had trained two people before she left. Past Perfect + before + Past Simple.

After she had finished her shift, she helped count stock. After + Past Perfect, Past Simple.

By the time she applied, she had worked there for two years. By the time + Past Simple, Past Perfect for the earlier action.

**Exercise: Choose the correct signal word**
- ___ the interviewer asked about her references, Gloria had already sent them. _(options: By the time / After)_
- ___ she had finished the paperwork, Jennifer shook her hand. _(options: After / By the time)_

**Exercise: Complete the sentence**
- When Jennifer asked for her references, Gloria ___ (already send) them by email.
- Before she walked into the interview, she ___ (practice) her answers with a friend.

## 4. Past Perfect vs. Past Simple

> 🖼 **Scene:** Loading dock. Diego calls his cousin during a break.  
> _Photo shows: Workers reviewing a clipboard together at a construction or warehouse site._

- **Diego:** I got the supervisor job! They called me this morning.
- **Cousin (phone):** No way! What did you tell them?
- **Diego:** I told them I had lifted heavy loads, had covered two shifts alone, and had never missed a day.

Use Past Simple for one finished action in the past. Use Past Perfect only when you have two past actions and need to show which one came first.

Common mistake: Using Past Perfect for a single action. ✗ "I had worked yesterday." (only one action, no sequence) ✓ "I worked yesterday."

**Exercise: One action or two?**
- Diego ___ the supervisor job this morning. (one fact, no sequence) _(options: got (Past Simple) / had got (Past Perfect))_
- Before the manager promoted him, Diego ___ every shift on time for six months. _(options: showed up (Past Simple) / had shown up (Past Perfect))_
- When Diego called his cousin, he ___ the news already. _(options: heard (Past Simple) / had already heard (Past Perfect))_

**Exercise: Error check**
- Which sentence is NOT correct? _(options: He had worked a double shift before he got promoted. / She had finished her break yesterday. / By the time the manager arrived, they had loaded the truck.)_

**Exercise: Fill in the correct tense**
- Diego ___ (work) at the warehouse for a year before he got the promotion.
- He ___ (call) his cousin right after the manager told him.

## 5. Your Work Story

> 🖼 **Scene:** Break room. Amara and her coworker Linh swap work histories.  
> _Photo shows: A small group of workers sitting together and talking during a break._

- **Amara:** Before I came here, I had done home care for four years. Nights mostly.
- **Linh:** I had worked in a restaurant before this. Twelve-hour shifts. I hadn't slept a full night in two years.
- **Amara:** That sounds hard. By the time I found this job, I had already saved enough to move. Now I walk here.
- **Linh:** Lucky you. And the pay is better here. The restaurant hadn’t paid overtime, not once.

Past Perfect is natural in work histories: you talk about what you had done before the current job, and what had already happened before a key moment.

**Exercise: Amara's story**

- By the time she found this job, she ___ (already save) enough to move.

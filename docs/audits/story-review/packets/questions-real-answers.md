## 1. Questions for smarter digital habits

> 🖼 **Scene:** East Boston. Rosa asks Sarah about an unexpected email.  
> _Photo shows: Adult students greeting each other and chatting before an evening class begins._

This week, use questions to talk about digital habits: checking messages, finding files, protecting your information, and asking for help. Rosa has an email that does not seem right.

Notice how Rosa and Sarah use questions to understand the message and decide what to do.

- **Rosa:** What do you do when you get a strange email?
- **Sarah:** I don't click anything. Who is it from?
- **Rosa:** It says it's from my bank. It wants me to click a link to keep my account open.
- **Sarah:** That sounds suspicious. Where do you usually check your account?
- **Rosa:** In the bank's app. I'll open that instead.
- **Sarah:** Good idea. If you're still worried, call the number on your bank card.

## 2. Who, What, Where: the basics

> 🖼 **Scene:** At a computer. Amara and Sarah look for a downloaded job application.  
> _Photo shows: A woman working on a laptop at a round table._

- **Amara:** I can't find the form I downloaded. Where is it?
- **Sarah:** Let's look in your Downloads folder. What is the file called?
- **Amara:** Job application. There it is! Who is the contact person?
- **Sarah:** James in the hiring office. His email address is on the job posting.
- **Amara:** Thanks. I'll check the address before I send it.

Questions with Who, What, Where use two different helpers depending on the verb.

With be: Who is the sender? Where are my downloads?

With other verbs: What do you share online? Where does she save her files?

The pattern:

Wh- word + am / is / are + subject

Where is the file? Who is the sender?

Wh- word + do / does + subject + base verb

What do you do? Where does she work?

Common mistake to avoid:

✗ Where you save your files? (missing do/does)

✓ Where do you save your files?

## 3. When and How: time and details

> 🖼 **Scene:** A community center in East Boston. Jean asks for help saving his photos.  
> _Photo shows: Two women talking in a modern office setting._

- **Tech helper:** When did you last save a copy of your photos on another device?
- **Jean:** I haven't done that. How do I copy them to my laptop?
- **Tech helper:** You can connect your phone with a USB cable. Do you have yours?
- **Jean:** Yes, right here. How long does it take?
- **Tech helper:** It depends on how many photos you have. Let's try a few first.
- **Jean:** Thanks. I don't want to lose my family photos if my phone breaks.

When asks about a time. How asks about a method or a detail.

How is powerful because it combines with other words to ask specific questions.

When did the message arrive? When does the download finish?

How do you join the video call? (through the app)

How long does the download take? (two minutes)

How often do you check your email? (twice a day)

How far is the computer from the Wi-Fi router? (ten feet)

**Exercise: When or How?**

- You want to know if the video is 6 minutes or 12 minutes. You ask: "___ is the video?" _(options: When / How long / How often)_
- You want to know if your classmate checks email every day or once a week. You ask: "___ do you check your email?" _(options: How far / How often / When)_

**Exercise: Fill in the question word**

## 4. Why: giving and asking for reasons

> 🖼 **Scene:** During a class break. Linh checks what an app can access on her phone.  
> _Photo shows: A group of young women standing around a classroom._

- **Linh:** Why does this flashlight app want my location?
- **Classmate:** That's strange. What happens if you say no?
- **Linh:** It still works. I'm turning off location access.
- **Classmate:** Why don't you use the flashlight that came with your phone?
- **Linh:** Because I didn't know I had one! Can you show me?

Why asks for a reason. The answer almost always starts with because.

Why + do/does/did/is/are + subject + verb?

Short answer (informal):

You do not have to repeat the full question in your answer.

Why do you silence your phone at night? Because I need to sleep.

## 5. Checking a suspicious message

> 🖼 **Scene:** After work in East Boston. Diego shows Fernanda an unexpected delivery text.  
> _Photo shows: A person holding a smartphone and using its on-screen keyboard._

Diego has not ordered anything, but a text says he owes a delivery fee. He and Fernanda talk through what to do before he clicks anything.

- **Diego:** I got a text about a package. It says I need to pay a delivery fee.
- **Fernanda:** What did you order?
- **Diego:** Nothing. That's why I'm confused.
- **Fernanda:** Then don't use the link. Where is the option to report the message?
- **Diego:** Here, under the menu. I'll report it as junk.
- **Fernanda:** Good. How often do you get these texts? I've had three this week.

All six question words work in the same basic pattern: Wh- word + helper verb (be/do/does/did) + subject + main verb. The helper verb changes. The order does not.

Two mistakes to watch for:

Missing helper verb

✗ Where you save your photos?

✓ Where do you save your photos?

Wrong word order (subject before helper)

✗ What you are downloading?

✓ What are you downloading?

## 1. "I'm Tired of Bending All Day"

> 🖼 **Scene:** East Boston. End of the afternoon shift. Tuesday.  
> _Photo shows: A housekeeping worker pushing a cart down a hotel hallway at the end of a long shift._

- **Elena:** I enjoy talking to the guests, but I hate how my back feels by 4 PM.
- **Amara:** I know. I've been avoiding lifting the heavy carts for weeks. My shoulder can't take it.
- **Elena:** I keep thinking about leaving, but I haven't said anything yet.
- **Amara:** Are you going to stay through spring?
- **Elena:** Probably. I can’t quit yet, but I want to look for something else.

Some verbs are always followed by a gerund (verb + -ing). You cannot use an infinitive after them.

Common mistake: ✗ "She enjoys to talk to guests." ✓ "She enjoys talking to guests."

## 2. "I Want to Try Something New"

> 🖼 **Scene:** The 116 bus. Tuesday evening. Elena and her cousin Bruno ride home.  
> _Photo shows: Two people talking on a city bus after work, looking out the window._

- **Bruno:** So are you really thinking about leaving housekeeping?
- **Elena:** Yeah. I want to try warehouse work. You said the pay is better.
- **Bruno:** It is. But you need to save a little first. Boots, gear. It adds up.
- **Elena:** I know. I decided to stay through spring. Then I'm going to look.
- **Bruno:** Smart. I can help you find a contact at my place.
- **Elena:** I would like to talk to someone there. That would really help.

Some verbs are always followed by an infinitive (to + base verb). You cannot use a gerund after them.

Common mistake: ✗ "She wants trying warehouse work." ✓ "She wants to try warehouse work."

## 3. Gerund or Infinitive? The Two Lists

> 🖼 **Scene:** East Boston Career Center. Thursday morning, before the afternoon shift.  
> _Photo shows: A person filling out a paper form at a desk in a career center waiting room._

Elena stops at the career center to pick up a job-skills form before she clocks in. She fills it out at a table by the door. Every question makes her think about what she actually does and wants.

The key question: which verb comes first? Check the list. Some verbs always take a gerund. Some always take an infinitive.

"I want to try warehouse work and plan to apply in the spring."

## 4. Both Are Right: start, like, love, hate

> 🖼 **Scene:** Hotel break room. Kevin checks the weekly schedule with Elena.  
> _Photo shows: Two coworkers having a quick conversation near a workplace break area._

- **Kevin (supervisor):** I'm putting you on mornings next week. I know you said you like working the early shift.
- **Elena:** Yes, I like to start early. It gives me time for the kids after school.
- **Kevin (supervisor):** Good. I personally hate staying past 5, so I get it.
- **Elena:** Same. I hate to miss pickup. It's already tight on Tuesdays.

A few verbs can take either a gerund or an infinitive. The meaning is the same at this level. Both are correct.

Remember: This is not true for most verbs. Enjoy, avoid, keep only take gerunds. Want, need, decide only take infinitives. The "both are fine" group is small.

**Exercise: Both correct or only one?**
- Kevin ___ leaving on time every day. _(options: loves / loves to leave (both correct) / only loves leaving (gerund only) / only loves to leave (infinitive only))_
- Elena ___ to clean the top-floor rooms. _(options: enjoys to clean (infinitive only) / enjoys cleaning (gerund only, enjoy never takes infinitive) / both are correct)_

## 5. Putting It Together: Talking About Your Job Goals

> 🖼 **Scene:** East Boston Adult Learning Center. Tuesday evening, after class.  
> _Photo shows: An adult student speaking one-on-one with a teacher after an evening English class._

After class, Elena stays behind to talk to Ms. Patel. She wants to practice saying out loud what she's been thinking about for months.

- **Ms. Patel:** Elena, you look distracted tonight. Everything okay?
- **Elena:** Sorry. I keep thinking about changing jobs. I want to try warehouse work, but I'm not ready to quit yet.
- **Ms. Patel:** What do you enjoy about your current job?
- **Elena:** I enjoy talking to the guests. And I like working early shifts. But I'm tired of the back pain. I avoid lifting as much as I can.
- **Ms. Patel:** What are you planning for the spring?
- **Elena:** I decided to stay through May. Then I plan to apply somewhere new. My cousin said he can help.
- **Ms. Patel:** That sounds like a plan. Let’s work on how you can describe your experience in an interview.

After class, a coworker from the hotel texted Elena a photo of a new café near the park. She invited Elena to have lunch after Saturday's shift.

The short version to remember:

enjoy / avoid / keep / finish / mind + verb + -ing

want / need / decide / plan / hope / would like + to + verb

like / love / hate / start + either form (both work)

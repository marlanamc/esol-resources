## 1. She Was Hired After the Referral

> 🖼 **Scene:** Outside a hotel near the airport, Thursday afternoon.  
> _Photo shows: Two women talking outside a workplace building, one sharing good news with a smile._

Gloria got a new job. Her cousin Marcos works at the same hotel and put in a good word for her. She tells her friend Linh the news.

- **Gloria:** I was hired! Marcos talked to his supervisor and I was called the same day.
- **Linh:** That's great! What did they say?
- **Gloria:** I was told to come in Monday with my ID. Most of the paperwork was done online.
- **Linh:** So you start next week? That was fast.

Notice what Gloria says: I was hired. I was called. I was told. She doesn't say who hired her or who called her. In these situations, the action matters more than the person who did it.

This is called the passive voice. It puts the receiver of the action first, not the person doing it.

Passive voice = the subject receives the action. We use it when we don't know who did something, or when it doesn't matter.

**Exercise: Active or passive?**
- "The manager called Marcos." _(options: Active: the manager is doing the action / Passive: the receiver comes first)_
- "Gloria was hired last week." _(options: Active: Gloria is doing the action / Passive: Gloria received the action)_
- "The paperwork was sent by email." _(options: Active / Passive: the paperwork received the action)_

## 2. Is Required / Is Provided: Reading the Job Ad

> 🖼 **Scene:** Job posting at the East Boston Career Center, Tuesday morning.  
> _Photo shows: A person reading a printed job posting at a community board._

Before she was hired, Gloria saw this job posting at the Career Center. Job ads almost always use passive voice. Look at the language:

Hotel Housekeeper, Full-Time

Experience in cleaning or hospitality is required.

Safety training is provided.

A uniform is included.

References are expected.

The hotel is located near the Blue Line.

All of these are present passive. The company doesn't say "We require experience" or "We provide training" because the company isn't the point. The job conditions are the point.

Present passive: is/are + V3 (past participle)

Singular: Experience is required. Plural: References are expected.

- **Gloria:** It says safety training is provided. So I don't have to pay for it?
- **Marcos:** Right. And a uniform is included too. You don't buy that either.

**Exercise: Choose the correct form**
- Non-slip shoes ___ for this position. _(options: is required / are required / require)_
- A background check ___ before your first day. _(options: is completed / are completed / completing)_
- "We require experience." This sentence in passive voice is: _(options: Experience is required. / Experience is requiring. / Experience was required.)_

## 3. Was Told / Was Given: First Day at Work

> 🖼 **Scene:** Hotel front desk, Monday morning. Gloria's first day.  
> _Photo shows: A new employee receiving orientation paperwork from a staff member at a front desk._

Monday is Gloria's first day. A lot happens. That night she calls her cousin to explain everything that happened to her.

- **Gloria:** I was given a locker and a uniform. Then I was told my schedule for the week.
- **Marcos:** Did they show you the break room?
- **Gloria:** Yes. Jennifer, the floor supervisor, showed me around. And I was asked to sign a form about the uniform.
- **Marcos:** Jennifer is good. You'll be fine.

These are all past passive. Something happened to Gloria. She received the action. The person who did it (Jennifer, HR) isn't always important.

Past passive: was/were + V3 (past participle)

Singular: She was given a locker. Plural: They were told the rules.

- "She was not pay for the extra hour." Is this sentence correct? _(options: Correct / Not correct. Should be 'was not paid' (V3))_

## 4. The Schedule Was Changed

> 🖼 **Scene:** Break room at the hotel, Thursday afternoon.  
> _Photo shows: A work schedule posted on a break room wall showing shift assignments._

Three weeks later, Gloria arranged to have Friday off for a family appointment. On Thursday she sees something on the break room wall: the schedule was changed. She texts her supervisor, Jennifer.

- **Gloria:** Did you see this? My Saturday shift was changed to Friday. I wasn't told anything.
- **Jennifer:** I know. Several shifts were moved without any notice. Can you work Friday?
- **Gloria:** No. My time off was approved two weeks ago. I have the email.
- **Jennifer:** Send it to HR and ask them to check. The procedure is posted in the handbook.

Passive voice comes up a lot in workplace problems. When something happens without explanation, workers often describe it passively: My schedule was changed. I wasn't told. We were moved.

Negative past passive: was not / wasn't + V3

I wasn't told. We weren't given any notice. She was not paid for Friday.

**Exercise: What does it mean?**
- "I wasn't told about the change." _(options: I did not tell someone about the change. / Nobody told me about the change. / I changed the schedule myself.)_
- "We were moved to a different floor." _(options: We moved ourselves to a different floor. / Someone moved us to a different floor. / We are going to move to a different floor.)_

**Exercise: Correct the error**
- "My check were not deposited on time." _(options: Correct / Not correct. Should be 'was not deposited' (singular subject: my check))_

## 5. Active or Passive? Choosing the Right One

> 🖼 **Scene:** Break room. Gloria reviews a copy of the employee handbook.  
> _Photo shows: A worker reading a company policy document at a table during a break._

Both active and passive voice are correct. The choice depends on what you want to focus on.

Use active when the doer is important or clear.

Use passive when the doer is unknown, obvious, or not the point.

- **Gloria:** The handbook says 'Overtime is calculated after 40 hours.' But I worked 43 and my check was short.
- **Jennifer:** Can I see your pay stub?
- **Gloria:** Here. Look, overtime is listed as zero hours.
- **Jennifer:** You should ask payroll to check. Tell them, “I worked 43 hours, but I was not paid for three hours of overtime.”

**Exercise: Active or passive? Choose the better sentence.**
- You don't know who changed the policy. Which sentence is better? _(options: Someone changed the break policy. / The break policy was changed.)_
- You want to talk about what your supervisor did. Which is better? _(options: Jennifer approved my time-off request. / My time-off request was approved by Jennifer.)_

**Exercise: Spot the error**
- "My overtime were not paid last Friday." _(options: Correct / Not correct. Should be 'was not paid' (overtime is singular))_
- "Safety training is provided to all new workers." _(options: Correct / Not correct)_

## 1. Zero Conditional: If It's Always True

> 🖼 **Scene:** La Palma Restaurant, East Boston. Monday morning before the lunch rush. The manager, Scott, checks his clipboard.  
> _Photo shows: Restaurant manager reviewing a clipboard near the kitchen entrance._

Carlos is a line cook at La Palma. He became a U.S. citizen in June, and this November is his first election. Election Day is Tuesday, November 3.

- **Carlos:** Scott, can I come in at 11 on Election Day? It's my first time voting.
- **Scott:** Congratulations. If you need a day off, you ask two weeks early. A morning is easier.
- **Carlos:** Just the morning. The polls open at 7. I'll be here by 11.
- **Scott:** Write it on the request sheet. My wife votes at 7 every year. If you go early, the line is short.
- **Carlos:** And if the line is long and I'm late?
- **Scott:** Same as any day. If you're running late, you call me before your shift starts.

Zero Conditional = a fact that is always true. A rule, a policy, a natural consequence. Both verbs are present simple. If + present simple, present simple.

The comma rule: If + condition first, then result. You can also flip it: You get to vote if you are in line by 8 PM. (No comma when the result comes first.)

- Which sentence is the Zero Conditional? _(options: If Carlos goes at 7, he will be at work by 11. / If Carlos went at 7, he would be at work by 11. / If you go early, the line is short.)_

## 2. First Conditional: Real Future Plans

> 🖼 **Scene:** Carlos and Ana's kitchen, Thursday night. Ana goes through the bills and the mail.  
> _Photo shows: Person reviewing bills and a paycheck at a kitchen table._

Carlos just got home from La Palma. His wife, Ana, is going through the mail.

- **Ana:** The electric bill came. It's $190 this month.
- **Carlos:** If I pick up an extra shift, I'll pay it. Anything else?
- **Ana:** Nothing from the city. Carlos, did you ever register to vote?
- **Carlos:** I thought they did that at my citizenship ceremony.
- **Ana:** Maybe not. If you don't register by October 24, you won't vote this year.
- **Carlos:** Get my wallet. If I do it online right now, it'll take five minutes.

First Conditional = a real situation that might happen. The result is in the future. If + present simple, will + base verb.

Important: In the if-part, never use will. Correct: If I pick up the shift... Wrong: If I will pick up the shift...

**Exercise: Real future or always true?**
- Carlos is talking about his specific plan for tonight. Which fits the First Conditional? _(options: If I register tonight, I vote in November. / If I register tonight, I will vote in November. / If I will register tonight, I will vote in November.)_
- Which sentence has an error? _(options: If the website asks for your license, read it to me. / If you miss the deadline, you won't vote this year. / If he will register tonight, he will vote in November.)_
- Ana tells her sister: If Carlos ___ (not work) on Election Day morning, he'll vote at 7.

## 3. Unless, When, and As Soon As

> 🖼 **Scene:** La Palma break room, the next Monday. Jennifer shows Carlos the November schedule on her phone.  
> _Photo shows: Two coworkers looking at a phone together in a break room._

Carlos is registered now. But Scott never saw the request sheet. Jennifer is a server at La Palma.

- **Carlos:** Look. I'm on a double on Election Day, 7 to close. I won't vote unless someone switches with me.
- **Jennifer:** Or you can vote early. The library has early voting on weekends. You don't need a reason.
- **Carlos:** I didn't know that. As soon as my registration goes through, I'll look up the times.
- **Jennifer:** I'll still ask Miguel about Tuesday. When he gets here, I'll ask him.
- **Carlos:** Thanks. But I won't say yes to him unless Scott agrees.

Unless = if not. Same tense rules as the First Conditional. When = at that moment in the future (more certain than if). As soon as = immediately when something happens.

Same rule: After unless, when, and as soon as, use present simple, not will. Correct: As soon as he gets here, I'll ask. Wrong: As soon as he will get here, I'll ask.

**Exercise: Unless, when, or as soon as?**
- Carlos means: 'If nobody switches with me, I won't be able to vote on Election Day.' Which sentence says this? _(options: I won't be able to vote when someone switches with me. / I won't be able to vote unless someone switches with me. / I won't be able to vote as soon as someone switches with me.)_
- Which sentence has an error? _(options: When the schedule posts, I'll text you. / As soon as Miguel will get here, I'll ask him. / I won't cover unless someone else steps up.)_

## 4. Zero vs. First: Which One Fits?

> 🖼 **Scene:** La Palma dining room, Thursday night. The dinner rush is on.  
> _Photo shows: Restaurant dining room busy during an evening shift._

Miguel can't switch. He has his kids on Tuesday. Carlos finds Scott between tables.

- **Carlos:** Scott, Miguel can't switch with me. So I'm voting early, Saturday morning at the library.
- **Scott:** Sorry about the schedule. Saturday morning's fine. Can you take Saturday night, too? We're short.
- **Carlos:** Saturday nights pay double, right?
- **Scott:** Yes. If you work Saturday nights, you earn double. Everybody wants those shifts.
- **Carlos:** Then if I vote Saturday morning, I'll still make the night shift. That covers the electric bill and helps with rent.
- **Scott:** Deal. If I don't see you by 5, I'll call you.

Zero vs. First: side by side

The key question: Is this always true (Zero) or is this Carlos's specific plan right now (First)? "You earn double on Saturday nights" = always true at La Palma. Zero. "I'll still make the night shift" = Carlos's plan for this Saturday. First.

## 5. Putting It Together: A Ride to the Polls

> 🖼 **Scene:** Carlos and Ana's living room, Friday evening. Carlos is at work. Ana's friend Beatriz stops by.  
> _Photo shows: Small apartment living room where two friends talk over bills and work schedules._

Beatriz lives downstairs. She is a citizen, but she doesn't drive. She cleans offices at night, and the polls close at 8.

- **Beatriz:** Ana, I want to vote this year. But I don't drive, and Tuesday I work until 9.
- **Ana:** Carlos is voting early tomorrow at the library. If you're ready at 9, he'll pick you up.
- **Beatriz:** Really? Then I'll be outside at 9. Are you coming too?
- **Ana:** I can't vote yet. I'm not a citizen. But if my sister comes for the kids, I can come with you.
- **Beatriz:** OK. Call me as soon as you know.
- **Ana:** Don't worry. My sister always says yes if I make her breakfast.

Which type is each sentence?

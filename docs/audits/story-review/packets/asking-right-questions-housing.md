## 1. What's the Place Like?

> 🖼 **Scene:** Meridian Street, East Boston. Rosa's break. She sees a listing taped to the laundromat window.  
> _Photo shows: Laundromat storefront with a rental flyer posted in the window._

- **Rosa:** Where is the apartment?
- **Brian:** It's on Leyden Street, near the bus stop.
- **Rosa:** How many bedrooms does it have?
- **Brian:** Two bedrooms.
- **Rosa:** What floor is it on?

Information questions start with a question word (Who, What, Where, When, Why, How). The verb comes before the subject: Does it have? not It does have?

The pattern:

Question word + do/does/is/are + subject + verb? Where is the apartment? How many bedrooms does it have? What floor is it on?

- "What floor is it on?" Is this question correct? _(options: Yes, correct. / No. Should be: 'What floor it is on?')_

## 2. How Much Is the Rent?

> 🖼 **Scene:** Rosa outside the laundromat. Brian picks up. She has five minutes before her shift starts.  
> _Photo shows: Woman on the phone outside a storefront window during a short work break._

- **Rosa:** How much is the rent?
- **Brian:** Fourteen hundred a month.
- **Rosa:** Is heat included?
- **Brian:** Heat and hot water are included. Electricity is extra.
- **Rosa:** Is there a laundry in the building?

How much asks about price or an uncountable amount. How many asks about a countable number. Both follow the same inversion rule: verb before subject.

**Exercise: Correct or not correct?**

- "Is heat included?" _(options: Correct / Not correct. Should be: Heat is included?)_
- "How much the security deposit is?" _(options: Correct / Not correct. Should be: How much is the security deposit?)_

## 3. Can You Tell Me If...?

> 🖼 **Scene:** Chelsea Street property management office. Nadine calls from her break at the hotel.  
> _Photo shows: Property management office reception desk with a staff member ready to assist._

- **Nadine:** Hello. Can you tell me what the monthly rent is?
- **Scott:** Sure. The two-bedroom apartment is $1,350 a month.
- **Nadine:** Do you know if heat is included?
- **Scott:** Yes, heat and hot water are both included.
- **Nadine:** Can you tell me whether parking spots are included?

Indirect questions are polite. After "Can you tell me" or "Do you know," the word order changes to statement order. No inversion.

Two patterns to use:

Can you tell me + what/where/when/how much + [subject + verb]? Do you know + if/whether + [subject + verb]?

**Exercise: Direct or indirect?**
- You want to ask about the deposit, politely. Which is correct? _(options: Can you tell me what is the deposit? / Can you tell me what the deposit is? / Can you tell me the deposit what is?)_
- You want to know if pets are allowed. Which is correct? _(options: Do you know if are pets allowed? / Do you know pets are allowed? / Do you know if pets are allowed?)_

**Exercise: Spot the word order mistake**
- "Can you tell me when is the apartment available?" _(options: Correct as is. / Can you tell me when the apartment is available? (statement order after 'tell me'))_
- "Do you know if the building has laundry?" _(options: Correct as is. / Do you know if has the building laundry?)_

## 4. Questions to Ask Before You Waste a Trip

> 🖼 **Scene:** Outside the hotel, East Boston. Rosa and Nadine compare calls after the evening shift.  
> _Photo shows: Two women talking outside on a city sidewalk in the evening after their work shift._

- **Rosa:** Did you ask about parking? I forgot.
- **Nadine:** I said: Can you tell me whether parking spots are included? He said yes.
- **Rosa:** Good. How long is the lease?
- **Nadine:** One year. He has appointments on Monday. Do you know when you can go?
- **Rosa:** Monday morning works. I have the day off. I’ll call him now.

Questions every renter should ask, direct and indirect:

How much is the rent? / Can you tell me how much the rent is?

Is heat included? / Do you know if heat is included?

Are parking spots included? / Can you tell me whether parking spots are included?

How long is the lease?

When can I see it? / Do you know when I can see it?

Is there laundry in the building?

How much is the security deposit?

Direct questions are fast and clear on the phone. Indirect questions sound polite at a formal office. Both are correct. Use the one that fits the situation.

**Exercise: Which question fits?**
- You are calling a private landlord quickly from the bus. Which question is better? _(options: Can you tell me what the parking fee is? / How much is the parking fee?)_
- You are at a property management office and want to ask about the lease. Which is better? _(options: How long is the lease? / Can you tell me how long the lease is?)_

**Exercise: Direct to indirect**
- Direct: "Are parking spots included?" Indirect version: _(options: Can you tell me if are parking spots included? / Can you tell me if parking spots are included? / Do you know parking spots are included?)_
- Direct: "When can I move in?" Indirect version: _(options: Do you know when can I move in? / Do you know when I can move in? / Can you tell me when I in can move?)_

## 1. Four tenses, one bus ride

> 🖼 **Scene:** Tuesday evening, 5:45 PM. Rosa heads to the 121 bus after work.  
> _Photo shows: Yellow and black city bus on a street between buildings._

Welcome back! Catch up with Rosa and David. Read for the message first.

- **David:** Hey, Rosa! Ready to be back?
- **Rosa:** Almost! Last night I was doing the dishes when my daughter asked, ‘Mom, where are my gym shoes?’
- **David:** First-day chaos?
- **Rosa:** Totally! We leave everything by the door on school nights. But last night? One shoe was still under the couch.
- **David:** That sounds like my house. How’s today going?
- **Rosa:** Better. My sister is helping with the kids this week. Now I just need to find MY notebook!

Time words give clues. Think about the situation.

These names help us talk about corrections. You don’t need to name the tense before speaking.

Present simple: a routine or stable fact. “We leave everything by the door on school nights.”

Present continuous: happening now or temporarily. “My sister is helping with the kids this week.”

Past simple: a completed past event. “My daughter asked about her gym shoes.”

Past continuous: in progress at a past moment. “I was doing the dishes.”

**Exercise: What is Rosa telling David?**
- Why is today easier for Rosa? _(options: The children don’t have school. / Her sister is helping with the kids this week. / She found her notebook.)_
- What was Rosa doing when her daughter asked about the gym shoes? _(options: Rosa was doing the dishes. / Rosa was riding the bus. / Rosa was working at the clinic.)_

## 2. Your usual routine · Present Simple

> 🖼 **Scene:** East Boston. Tuesday evening after work.  
> _Photo shows: City skyline with high-rise buildings._

- **Classmate:** You always have snacks in that bag!
- **Rosa:** Of course. My daughter gets hungry the minute we leave the house.
- **Classmate:** Same with my son. He says, ‘I’m not hungry.’ Then he eats my sandwich.
- **Rosa:** Exactly! I pack two now. One for her and one for me.

Rosa and her classmate know this routine well. What do you always carry in your bag?

Quick form check: I/you/we/they work; he/she/it works.

**Exercise: Tell someone about the routine**
- Rosa says, “I pack a snack every day. My daughter eat it on the bus.” Fix the second sentence. _(options: My daughter eat it on the bus. / My daughter eating it on the bus. / My daughter eats it on the bus.)_
- “My son always ___ for a snack as soon as we get on the bus.” (ask)
- “We ___ two sandwiches every morning. One is never enough!” (pack)

## 3. What’s happening now? · Present Continuous

> 🖼 **Scene:** Before class, Rosa gets a message from her sister.  
> _Photo shows: empty classroom with desks and chalkboard_

- **Rosa's sister:** Quick question. Where’s the rice?
- **Rosa:** Top shelf, next to the pasta. What are you making?
- **Rosa's sister:** I’m making dinner. Your daughter is helping me. Well, she’s eating some pineapple.
- **Rosa:** Ha! Save some for me. I’m hungry already.

Rosa’s sister is making dinner while Rosa waits for class. What’s happening at your home right now?

Quick form check: use am/is/are + verb-ing: “I’m making dinner.”

**Exercise: Give an update**
- Rosa’s sister sends a photo from the kitchen: “Your daughter ___ all the pineapple!” (eat)
- Rosa’s sister usually works evenings. This week she’s off. Which message tells Rosa about her help just for this week? _(options: I make dinner for your kids every week. / I’m making dinner for your kids this week. / I made dinner for your kids last week.)_
- Her sister texts: “We’re in the kitchen. We ___ dinner now. Come hungry!” (make)

## 4. Tell a past story · Past Simple and Continuous

> 🖼 **Scene:** First morning back. Two different shoes.  
> _Photo shows: A pair of red and yellow sneakers side by side._

- **Teacher:** How was the first morning back? Everyone on time?
- **Student:** Almost. We were waiting for the bus when my son looked down at his feet. Two different shoes.
- **Teacher:** Oh no! What did you do?
- **Student:** We ran home. I found his other shoe under the sofa. He made the bus!

They were already waiting for the bus when the boy noticed his shoes. Then they ran home.

Quick form check: past simple uses a past form, such as looked or found. Past continuous uses was/were + verb-ing.

“Yesterday” can go with either tense: “Yesterday I cooked dinner.” “Yesterday at six, I was cooking dinner.” The speaker’s meaning matters.

**Exercise: Two different shoes**
- The bus wasn’t there yet. “We ___ at the stop when my son noticed his shoes.” (wait)
- “We ran home. I ___ the other shoe under the sofa and gave it to him.” (find)
- “My son was still getting ready when the bus arrived. He ___ on his shoe.” (put)

## 5. Your turn: catch up, check, try again

First, try it with support. Say the replies aloud or write them in your notebook. Use your own details.

- **Classmate:** What’s one thing you always do before you leave home?
- **You:** I usually …
- **Classmate:** Anything different at home or work this week?
- **You:** This week, I am …
- **Classmate:** Any surprises this week? Something you forgot, a funny moment, or a change of plans?
- **You:** I was … when …

Now make it yours. Look away from the starters. Catch up with a partner, speak aloud to yourself, or write a short message: share your routine, something temporary, and a past story. You can invent details.

Say it → check one sentence → say it again.

“I doing the dishes when my daughter asked about her gym shoes.”

I mean the dishes were not finished yet. Past continuous needs was/were.

“I was doing the dishes when my daughter asked about her gym shoes.”

Choose just one sentence from your catch-up. Does it express your meaning? Use the tense reference if you need help checking the verb. Then say or write the sentence again. If it already works, keep it!

## 1. Where Are You Going?

> 🖼 **Scene:** Maverick Blue Line Station, East Boston. Tuesday, 7:00 PM.  
> _Photo shows: Woman standing outside a subway station entrance at dusk, looking at her phone._

Amara cleans offices downtown. She waited three weeks for this doctor's appointment at the clinic on Meridian Street. It's at 7:15. If she is more than 10 minutes late, she has to make a new appointment and wait three more weeks. She has never been to this clinic, so she calls her coworker Jean.

- **Amara:** Jean, I'm at Maverick. My appointment is at 7:15. How do I get to the clinic?
- **Jean:** Walk across Maverick Square. Turn left on Meridian Street.
- **Amara:** Wait. I got a text. It says my package can't be delivered, and there's a link. I'm not expecting a package.
- **Jean:** That's a scam. Delete it. Use your map, not the link.
- **Amara:** Deleted. OK, I'm on Meridian. Then what?
- **Jean:** Cross at the light. Go two more blocks. It's on the right. Now hurry.

Imperatives = commands and instructions. Use the base form of the verb. There is no subject. You are talking directly to the person.

No subject needed. You do not say "You turn left." Just say "Turn left." The verb is first.

## 2. How to Say "Don't"

> 🖼 **Scene:** Meridian Street, 7:07 PM. Handwritten notes on the board next to the clinic's front door.  
> _Photo shows: Close-up of handwritten notes and papers pinned to a community bulletin board._

Amara gets to the clinic at 7:07. The front door is locked. Jean always comes in the daytime, so she didn't know. There are notes on a board next to the door.

Evening patients: from Claudette, Office Manager

The front door locks at 7 PM. Don't knock. Nobody can hear you.

Walk around the corner to Paris Street and use the side entrance.

Don't use the side entrance after 8 PM. It locks automatically.

Please ask Laura at the front desk if you need help.

Please sign in when you arrive. Thank you!

Negative imperative: Don't + base verb. Use it to tell someone NOT to do something. Polite imperative: Please + base verb. Same structure, softer tone.

**Exercise: Don't or please?**
- Claudette wants to warn patients NOT to do something. Which sentence does she write? _(options: Park in front of the gate. / Don't park in front of the gate. / Not park in front of the gate.)_
- Claudette wants to give a polite instruction about phones. Which is best? _(options: You should turn off your phone in the waiting room. / Don't turn off your phone in the waiting room. / Please turn off your phone in the waiting room.)_

**Exercise: Check the sign**
- "Please asks Laura at the front desk if you need help." Is this correct? _(options: Correct / Not correct. Should be "Please ask" (base verb, no -s))_

**Exercise: Write the warning**

## 3. Where Is It?

> 🖼 **Scene:** Paris Street, 7:09 PM. Amara stops Linh on the sidewalk outside the laundromat.  
> _Photo shows: Two neighbors talking on a city sidewalk with storefronts in the background._

Amara walks around the corner to Paris Street. She sees shops, a laundromat and two doors, but no clinic sign. Six minutes left.

- **Amara:** Excuse me. I'm looking for the side entrance to the clinic. Do you know where it is?
- **Linh:** Yes. It's next to the pharmacy, across from this laundromat.
- **Amara:** I see a green door and a blue door. Which one?
- **Linh:** The green one. It's between the pharmacy and the bakery. There's no sign. Everybody gets lost.
- **Amara:** Thank you so much. My appointment is in six minutes.

Prepositions of location tell you WHERE something is in relation to something else.

## 4. Step by Step

> 🖼 **Scene:** The bus stop on Meridian Street, near the clinic. Elena will get off the bus here next Tuesday.  
> _Photo shows: A city bus stop on a busy urban street with pedestrians walking past._

Amara opens the green door at 7:13 and signs in. Laura at the front desk looks at the clock. "Two minutes early," she says. After the appointment, Amara texts her neighbor Elena. Elena has an appointment at the same clinic next Tuesday at 7:30, and she comes by bus.

Amara's text to Elena

First, walk straight on Meridian Street for two blocks.

Then, turn left on Paris Street. The front door is locked after 7.

Next, go past the laundromat and cross at the crosswalk.

Finally, look for the green door. It's on the right, next to the pharmacy. There's no sign.

P.S. If you get a text about a package, don't tap the link. It's a scam.

Use First, Then, Next, Finally to put directions in order. Each step starts with an imperative + preposition to say what to do AND where.

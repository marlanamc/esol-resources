## 1. The wrong lunch bag · five verb forms

> 🖼 **Scene:** Tuesday, class break. Carlos opens his lunch bag and finds a surprise.  
> _Photo shows: People sitting inside a classroom during an evening lesson._

Catch up with Carlos and Fernanda. Read for the message first.

- **Fernanda:** Break time. What did you bring for dinner tonight?
- **Carlos:** A sandwich. Wait, this isn’t my bag. There’s no food, just a little box.
- **Fernanda:** Is that a ring box? Whose bag is that?
- **Carlos:** I took it from the staff fridge at the restaurant. Mark, the line cook, works there with my sister.
- **Fernanda:** Call your sister. Maybe Mark is looking for it.
- **Carlos:** I can’t. Sarah’s working right now. She’s a server, so I’ll text her.

Carlos preps food at La Palma in the mornings, so he uses the staff fridge too. Notice works and working, then compare all five forms below.

Five labels, one verb. The labels help you find and check a form.

V1: work · the base form

V1-s: works · with he/she/it in the present

V-ing: working · as in “She’s working now.”

V2: worked · a past form

V3: worked · as in “I’ve worked that shift.”

Try it: Say the five forms. Look away and try again. Check just the one you forget.

**Exercise: Know the codes**
- Why is Carlos surprised at the break? _(options: He is late for his restaurant shift. / His bag has a ring box, not his dinner. / His sister forgot her lunch at home.)_
- Can Carlos call Sarah right now? _(options: No. She is working. / Yes. She has finished work. / Yes. She is at home.)_
- Carlos tells Fernanda: “Mark ___ at the restaurant with my sister.” (work)

## 2. Mark is looking everywhere · V1, V1-s, V-ing

> 🖼 **Scene:** Still on break, Carlos texts his sister Sarah at the restaurant.  
> _Photo shows: Busy restaurant dining room with staff serving during an evening shift._

- **Carlos:** Emergency! I took the wrong lunch bag from the fridge. There’s a ring inside. Is it Mark’s?
- **Sarah:** Yes. Mark brings that blue bag every day. And tonight he wants to ask Lisa to marry him.
- **Carlos:** Oh no. Class finishes at eight. Where is he now?
- **Sarah:** He’s looking everywhere in the kitchen. I’m serving tables, so I can’t leave. Meet us at the café at eight.

Sarah talks about Mark’s usual routine and what is happening right now. Have you ever taken the wrong bag, coat, or phone?

Quick form check: I/you/we/they bring; he/she/it brings. For an action in progress, use am/is/are + V-ing: “He’s looking everywhere.”

Your reply: “I usually ___ before class. Right now, I’m ___.” Use your own details, then say it again without looking.

**Exercise: Spot the form**
- Sarah is busy at this moment. Which text tells Carlos what is happening now? _(options: I serve tables every evening. / I served tables last night. / I’m serving tables. I’ll text you later.)_
- Sarah texts: “Mark never ___ his lunch bag. He’s checking every shelf!” (forget)
- Carlos replies: “Tell him not to worry. I am ___ an alarm for eight o’clock.” (set)

## 3. Just in time · V2 and V3

> 🖼 **Scene:** 8:05 at the café. Mark runs in, out of breath.  
> _Photo shows: Cozy neighborhood café with warm light, tables, and people relaxing after work._

- **Mark:** Carlos! I’ve looked everywhere for that bag!
- **Carlos:** Sorry. I grabbed the wrong one this morning. I opened it at break. Here’s your ring.
- **Mark:** Thank you. Now I have to tell you something. I ate your sandwich at lunch.
- **Fernanda:** So Carlos has missed dinner, but he saved your big night. Go, Mark! You have twenty minutes.

Carlos tells what happened this morning. Mark and Fernanda connect the past to right now. The examples below show how V2 and V3 work with the same verb.

Quick form check: “I grabbed the bag this morning” uses V2. “I’ve looked everywhere” uses have + V3. I’ve means I have.

For regular verbs, V2 and V3 both end in -ed. They look the same; the words around them help you understand the meaning.

Try it: Tell a partner one thing you did yesterday. Check the verb and tell it again. You can invent details.

**Exercise: Choose V2 or V3**
- After Mark leaves, Fernanda says, “I’ve worked at the clinic for two years.” What does she mean? _(options: She stopped working there two years ago. / She started two years ago and still works there. / She will start there in two years.)_
- Carlos tells Mark: “I ___ class with your ring in my bag!” (start)
- Mark laughs: “I have ___ that blue bag for three years. I need a new one!” (use)

## 4. She said yes! · Be and have

> 🖼 **Scene:** Later that night, Sarah starts a group chat with everyone from the café.  
> _Photo shows: A student using a laptop to complete an online lesson at a desk._

- **Sarah:** Big news. Mark is engaged. Lisa said yes at 8:30!
- **Carlos:** I am so happy for them. I’m also very hungry.
- **Mark:** Lisa has the ring, and I have a sandwich for you tomorrow. Thank you, Carlos!
- **Fernanda:** You were a hero tonight, Carlos. Take the right bag tomorrow.
- **Carlos:** I’ve been careful all night. I’ve had enough surprises for one week!

Be and have change in special ways. Don’t add -ed.

Be: V1 be · V1-s is · V-ing being · V2 was/were · V3 been

Have: V1 have · V1-s has · V-ing having · V2 had · V3 had

In present statements, use I am, he/she/it is, you/we/they are.

Join the chat: “Today I am ___. Yesterday I was ___. I have ___.” Say or write your own reply. You can invent details.

Check one thing: “Lisa have the ring.” → “Lisa has the ring.” With she, use has. Now check one verb in your reply and try again. Return to two forms tomorrow.

**Exercise: be and have on the quiz**
- Sarah types, “Lisa have the ring now.” Help her fix the message. _(options: Lisa has the ring now. / Lisa having the ring now. / Lisa have the ring now.)_
- Fernanda replies: “We ___ at the café when Mark ran in!” (be)
- Carlos texts: “I’ve ___ a crazy day. Five minutes of practice is enough tonight.” (have)

## 1. What If? Imagining Something Different

> 🖼 **Scene:** East Boston restaurant kitchen. Thursday night, end of shift.  
> _Photo shows: Restaurant kitchen at the end of a shift, stainless steel counters and a cook wiping down the line._

- **Carmen:** My manager just told me I'm working Sundays now. Every Sunday.
- **Marta:** Every Sunday? What would you do if you could choose your own schedule?
- **Carmen:** If I had more options, I would look for a different job. But right now I need this one.
- **Marta:** I understand. If my pay were better, I wouldn’t need two jobs. It’s hard to make a change.

Second conditional = an imaginary situation and its result. The situation is unlikely or not real right now.

Carmen does not have many options right now. She is imagining a different situation that is not real. That is the second conditional.

The verb after if looks like past tense, but it does not talk about the past. It shows the situation is imaginary.

**Exercise: Real or imaginary?**
- "If I had a car, I would take the early shift." (Carmen doesn't have a car.) _(options: Real plan / Imaginary situation)_
- "If the bus arrives on time, I will clock in before 7." (The bus usually comes.) _(options: Real plan / Imaginary situation)_
- "If I worked days, I would see my kids after school." (She works nights.) _(options: Real plan / Imaginary situation)_

**Exercise: Complete the sentence**
- If Carmen ___ (have) Sundays off, she would go to her sister's house.
- If the manager ___ (listen), the whole team would be happier.

## 2. Building the Sentence

> 🖼 **Scene:** Chelsea Street warehouse break room. Friday morning.  
> _Photo shows: Warehouse worker in a hard hat walking through a large storage facility with shelving and boxes._

- **Ramon:** If my schedule changed to nights, I would need a different babysitter.
- **Scott:** What would you do if they moved you to a different site?
- **Ramon:** If the new site were far, I wouldn't take it. Two buses is too much.

Structure: If + past simple (if-clause) + comma + would + base verb (main clause). You can also put the main clause first: I would need a babysitter if my schedule changed. No comma needed when if comes second.

## 3. What Would You Do? Advice at Work

> 🖼 **Scene:** Home health agency office, East Boston. Monday afternoon. Beatriz calls Nadine from a client's home.  
> _Photo shows: Small office waiting area with chairs, a reception desk, and afternoon light coming through a window._

- **Beatriz:** My coworker didn't show up for Mrs. Lopez again. She's alone right now. What would you do?
- **Nadine:** If I were you, I would call the agency right now, not wait.
- **Beatriz:** And if they cut my hours because I called?
- **Nadine:** If they did that, I would talk to the supervisor and write it down.

"If I were you..." is the most common way to give advice in English. Use were (not was) in formal and standard written English for all subjects in the if-clause when the situation is imaginary.

**Exercise: Spot the error**
- "If I am you, I would call the agency." _(options: Correct as written / Not correct. Should be: If I were you, I would call the agency. / Not correct. Should be: If I was you, I would call the agency.)_
- "If I were the supervisor, I would change the policy." _(options: Correct / Not correct. Should be 'was')_

**Exercise: Give advice**
- If I ___ you, I would ask for that in writing.
- If I were you, I ___ (not sign) that form without reading it.

## 4. Real vs. Imaginary: First vs. Second Conditional

> 🖼 **Scene:** Hotel housekeeping, East Boston. Friday morning.  
> _Photo shows: Hotel corridor with a housekeeping cart parked outside a guest room door._

- **Kevin:** If there's overtime tomorrow, I'll take it. I need the money.
- **Sofia:** I wish I could. If it weren't my son's birthday, I would take it too.
- **Kevin:** If they offered Sunday instead, would you do it?
- **Sofia:** Maybe. If the pay were double, I definitely would.

First conditional: If + present simple, will + base verb. The situation is real and possible. Second conditional: If + past simple, would + base verb. The situation is imaginary or unlikely.

**Exercise: First or second conditional?**
- Kevin always picks up extra shifts. "If they ___ me overtime next week, I'll say yes." _(options: offer (first conditional, real possibility) / offered (second conditional, imaginary))_
- Sofia can't change her son's birthday. "If I ___ free on Saturday, I would come in." _(options: am (first conditional, real possibility) / were (second conditional, imaginary))_
- "If the manager calls before noon, I would go in early." Is this sentence correct? _(options: Correct / Not correct. Mixing tenses: should be 'calls...will go' or 'called...would go')_

**Exercise: Complete the sentence**
- If Sofia finishes by 3, she ___ pick up her son on time. (real plan)
- If Kevin had a second job, he ___ save faster. (imaginary)

## 5. At Work: What Would Change?

> 🖼 **Scene:** Office cleaning company break room. Late at night.  
> _Photo shows: Office building hallway at night, empty desks visible through glass partitions and a cleaning cart in the corridor._

- **Milagros:** If they promoted me to team lead, I would train the new people better.
- **Jennifer:** What else would you change if you were in charge?
- **Milagros:** If we had the same schedule every week, people wouldn't quit so fast.
- **Jennifer:** Exactly. And if they paid us more, I wouldn’t need a second job. I could be more flexible here.

Use longer if-clauses to talk about bigger changes: If the whole system were different... Use the second conditional any time you imagine a situation that is not real right now.

**Exercise: What would Milagros do?**
- Milagros does not have a promotion right now. "If the company ___ me team lead, I would train new workers." _(options: promotes / promoted / will promote)_
- "If they pay everyone the same, I wouldn't need two jobs." Is this correct? _(options: Correct / Not correct. Should be 'paid' to match 'wouldn't need')_

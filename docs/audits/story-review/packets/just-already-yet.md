## 1. She's already done it: ALREADY

> 🖼 **Scene:** East Boston Community Center, front desk. Tuesday morning.  
> _Photo shows: Bright community center reception area with a front desk and welcoming entrance._

Nadine stops by the front desk before class.

- **Staff:** Hi, are you here to sign up for the food pantry?
- **Nadine:** No, I have already signed up. I came in last week.
- **Staff:** Oh, good. Is there anything else I can help with?
- **Nadine:** Yes, actually. I haven't gotten my pickup day yet.

ALREADY = done before now, sooner than expected, or to confirm something is finished. It does not tell you when it happened.

Where does ALREADY go?

ALREADY sits between have / has and the past participle (V3). It never moves to the end.

Common mistake

✗ She has signed up already. (already at the end. not the standard position)

✓ She has already signed up.

## 2. Just finished: JUST

> 🖼 **Scene:** Pharmacy on Chelsea Street. Thursday afternoon.  
> _Photo shows: A neighborhood pharmacy counter with shelves of medicine and a prescription pickup window._

- **Mark:** Hey Carlos, coming back from the pharmacy?
- **Carlos:** Yeah, I have just picked up my prescription. The line was crazy.
- **Mark:** I know. I've just gotten back from there too. Did they have everything?
- **Carlos:** Yes, thankfully. They have just restocked my medicine, so I didn’t have to come back.

JUST = very recently, a short time ago. The action finished moments ago or today.

Where does JUST go?

Like ALREADY, JUST sits between have / has and the past participle (V3).

JUST vs. ALREADY

Common mistake

✗ I just have picked up my prescription. (just before have = wrong)

✓ I have just picked up my prescription.

**Exercise: JUST or ALREADY?**
- Carlos is still on the stairs. He got back from the pharmacy two minutes ago. Which fits? _(options: have JUST gotten back. moments ago / have ALREADY gotten back. done before)_
- The pharmacist calls your name. You say: "I ___ arrived." (a few seconds ago) _(options: have just / have already)_
- Your neighbor asks if you've been to the pharmacy this week. You went on Monday. You say: _(options: I have just been. / I have already been.)_

**Exercise: Fix the mistake**
- "Carlos just has picked up his medication." _(options: Correct as written / Not correct. Should be: Carlos has just picked up his medication.)_
- "They have just restocked the shelves." _(options: Correct as written / Not correct)_

## 3. Not yet: YET

> 🖼 **Scene:** Linh's kitchen table, East Boston. Wednesday evening.  
> _Photo shows: Kitchen table with a phone, papers, and a cup of coffee. A person organizing their week._

- **Linh's son:** Mom, have you called the landlord yet?
- **Linh:** No, I haven't called him yet. I will do it tomorrow.
- **Linh's son:** OK. Have you paid the electric bill yet? It’s due tomorrow.
- **Linh:** Not yet. Let me do that now, before I forget.

YET = something expected or planned, but not done so far. Used in questions and negative sentences.

Where does YET go?

YET always goes at the end of the sentence. It never sits between have and V3.

YET appears in two patterns only:

Negative

Question

Common mistakes

✗ She has yet called the landlord. (yet between have and V3 = wrong)

✗ She has called the landlord yet. (yet in an affirmative sentence = wrong)

✓ She hasn't called the landlord yet.

**Exercise: Where does YET go?**
- Which sentence is correct? _(options: She has yet emailed the plumber. / She hasn't emailed the plumber yet. / She hasn't yet the plumber emailed.)_
- Linh's son wants to know about the library books. Which question is correct? _(options: Have you yet returned the library books? / Have you returned the library books yet? / Yet have you returned the library books?)_
- "I haven't had time ___." Which word completes the sentence correctly? _(options: yet. goes at the end / already. goes between have and V3 / just. goes between have and V3)_
- I haven't called the landlord ___.

## 5. Hear it, use it: Practice in the real world

> 🖼 **Scene:** Quick exchanges. East Boston, around the neighborhood.  
> _Photo shows: A residential apartment hallway with numbered doors and natural light at the end of the corridor._

These are real phrases you might hear or say this week, on the bus, at the store, or talking with a neighbor. Read them and check your understanding.

- **Amara:** Bruno, have you signed up for the food pantry yet?
- **Bruno:** Yes, I have already registered. I did it online last week.
- **Amara:** Oh nice. I have just found out about it. I'll do it tonight.
- **Bruno:** Check the dates first. This month’s deadline has just passed, I think. You might need to wait until next month.

Quick summary before the quiz:

**Exercise: What does it mean?**
- "Amara hasn't registered for the food pantry yet." _(options: Amara registered a long time ago. / Amara registered just now. / Amara hasn't registered, but she probably will.)_
- "Amara has just found out about the program." _(options: Amara learned about the program a long time ago. / Amara learned about the program very recently. / Amara doesn't know about the program yet.)_
- "I have already called the landlord." What does this tell you? _(options: The call happened before now. It is done. / The call is happening right now. / The call hasn't happened yet.)_

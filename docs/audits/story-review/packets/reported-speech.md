## 2. Exam room: direct vs reported

> 🖼 **Scene:** 9:10 AM. Dr. Chen checks Mina's numbers on her laptop.  
> _Photo shows: A clinician in scrubs with a laptop during a medical visit._

In the exam room, Mina hears direct speech (the exact words). Tonight, on the phone, she uses reported speech to retell it.

- **Dr. Chen:** You're doing better with your blood pressure. But your A1c is higher than in March.
- **Mina:** Higher? I don't eat sweets. Well, only the cookies in the hotel break room.
- **Dr. Chen:** You're also a little dehydrated. You need to drink more water and walk more.
- **Mina:** I walk all day! I clean sixteen rooms a shift.
- **Dr. Chen:** A walk after dinner is different. We'll do a fasting blood test and look at your medicine.

Tonight, Mina tells Gloria: Dr. Chen said I was doing better with my blood pressure, but my A1c was higher.

Direct speech

Exact words (quotation marks)

Dr. Chen said, "You need to walk more."

Pharmacist Sam said, "Take this pill at night."

Reported speech

Retelling: no quotes, and tenses often shift back

Dr. Chen said I needed to walk more.

Sam told me to take the pill at night.

What usually changes

No quotation marks

Tenses shift back (present → past, will → would)

Pronouns change (I → she, you → I)

Time words change (today → that day, tomorrow → the next day)

## 3. Front desk: say vs tell

> 🖼 **Scene:** 8:40 AM. Check-in at the front desk. Alex helps the patient ahead of Mina first.  
> _Photo shows: A patient in a wheelchair at a clinic reception desk._

- **Alex:** Good morning, Mina. Your copay is thirty dollars today.
- **Mina:** Okay. Here's my card.
- **Alex:** Hmm. This card is expired in our system. Please bring your new one next time.
- **Mina:** The new one is on my fridge at home. Of course.

Alex gave Mina two facts. To report them, English uses said and told a little differently.

SAY: the listener is optional

Subject + said + (that) + statement

Alex said (that) the copay was thirty dollars.

Alex said to me (that) the copay was thirty dollars.

Both are correct. If you name the listener after said, you need to: said to me.

Never: ❌ Alex said me that…

TELL: the listener is required

Subject + told + (me/you/him/her/us/them) + (that) + statement

Alex told me (that) my insurance card was expired.

He told us (that) the lab was running late.

You must name the listener: ❌ He told that…

## 4. After vitals: tense backshift

> 🖼 **Scene:** 9:40 AM. In Bay 2, Nurse Jordan explains the fasting rules on a card.  
> _Photo shows: A healthcare professional explaining something while holding a card or paper._

- **Jordan:** Bad news. The lab is at capacity now, so you can't do the blood test today.
- **Mina:** But I took the whole morning off for this.
- **Jordan:** I know. I can get you in tomorrow at 7:30 AM. You must fast tonight. Water is okay.
- **Mina:** My shift starts at 8. My boss is going to love this.
- **Jordan:** The test is quick, but the lab is busy. With the wait, it takes about an hour. I'll call you tonight.

Tonight, Jordan's words are already in the past. When Mina reports them, English often moves the verbs "one step back" in time: Jordan said I had to fast that night.

Time words

today → that day

tomorrow → the next day

yesterday → the day before

now → then

## 5. Pharmacy: commands and requests

> 🖼 **Scene:** 10:15 AM. Old bottles line the shelves at the pharmacy next to the clinic while Mina waits for Sam.  
> _Photo shows: Shelves lined with many glass and plastic bottles in a historic pharmacy._

- **Sam:** Dr. Chen added a new cholesterol pill. Start it after the test. Take it at night. Don't drink grapefruit juice with it.
- **Mina:** Grapefruit? Is that a joke?
- **Sam:** No joke. Grapefruit can make this pill too strong. Please read this warning before you go.
- **Mina:** Okay. No grapefruit. That's easy. I never buy it.

Sam's instructions and requests use a different shape when Mina reports them: told / asked + person + to + base verb.

Commands

told + me + to + base verb

Direct: "Take this with food." → Sam told me to take it with food.

Direct: "Don't drink grapefruit juice." → Sam told me not to drink grapefruit juice.

Polite requests

asked + me + to + base verb

Direct: "Please read this warning." → Sam asked me to read the warning.

## 9. Visit summary: quick reference

> 🖼 **Scene:** Checkout: the visit summary on a tablet. Here is Mina's version of the rules.  
> _Photo shows: A clinician holding a tablet during a consultation with a patient._

Say vs tell

Say: said (that) + statement, or said to + person + (that) + statement

Tell: told + person + (that) + statement

Never: said me… / told that…

Statements

Subject + said/told + (that) + backshifted clause

"Your A1c is higher." → Dr. Chen said my A1c was higher.

"I will call you tonight." → Jordan said she would call me that night.

Commands / requests

told/asked + person + to + verb · negative: not to + verb

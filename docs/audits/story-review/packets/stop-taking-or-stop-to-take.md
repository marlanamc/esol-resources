## 1. Stop Taking the Old Medicine

> 🖼 **Scene:** East Boston Pharmacy on Meridian Street, Thursday 10:15 PM.  
> _Photo shows: A pharmacist at the counter handing a medication bag to a customer late at night._

Marisol just finished her closing shift at the warehouse. She stops at the pharmacy on the way home. The pharmacist, Jennifer, has her new prescription ready, but there's something important she needs to know.

- **Jennifer:** Marisol, the doctor changed your prescription. You need to stop taking the old blood pressure pills tonight.
- **Marisol:** Stop taking them? Even if I still have some at home?
- **Jennifer:** Yes. Stop taking them completely. Don't mix the two medications.

Marisol texts her daughter on the bus home.

- **Marisol:** Mija, I got my new prescription. Tell your father I need to stop taking the old white pills.
- **Daughter:** OK Mamá. I'll tell him when he gets home.

In both messages, stop taking refers to Marisol’s old pills. That means: she was taking the pills before, and now she needs to end that habit.

stop + -ing = end an ongoing action. You were doing it. Now you stop.

She stopped taking the old pills. → She was taking them. Now she stopped.

- "He stopped working at the warehouse." _(options: He went to the warehouse in order to work. / He was working there, and now he is not. / He will start working at the warehouse.)_

## 2. Stop to Take It With Food

> 🖼 **Scene:** Break room at the warehouse, Friday lunchtime.  
> _Photo shows: a man and a woman in a warehouse_

The next day at work, Kevin checks whether Marisol is ready to return from break. She explains why she paused and clears up a misunderstanding about her new prescription.

- **Kevin:** Are you coming back to the floor? Our break is nearly over.
- **Marisol:** In a minute. I stopped to take my new pill. The label says to take it with food.
- **Kevin:** Oh, I thought the doctor told you to stop taking your pills.
- **Marisol:** Just the old ones. I stopped taking those yesterday. This is the new prescription.

Kevin learns why Marisol paused. Stop + to + verb means you pause your current activity in order to do a new one. Two different actions.

stop + to + verb = pause what you're doing in order to do something else. Two actions.

She stopped to take her pill. → She was doing something. She paused. She took the pill.

**Exercise: Which meaning?**
- "Marisol stopped to take her pill at noon." _(options: Marisol ended the habit of taking the pill. / Marisol paused what she was doing in order to take the pill. / Marisol forgot to take the pill.)_
- "Kevin stopped eating to answer his phone." _(options: Kevin quit eating forever. / Kevin paused his meal to answer the phone. / Kevin ate and answered the phone at the same time.)_

**Exercise: Stop taking or stop to take?**
- The doctor told her to ___ the old medication. (end the habit) _(options: stop taking / stop to take)_
- She ___ her lunch ___ her pill. (she paused eating, then took the pill) _(options: stopped eating / to take / stopped eating / taking)_

## 3. Try Taking the Generic / Try to Get Covered

> 🖼 **Scene:** Pharmacy aisle, Friday evening. Marisol compares two medicine boxes.  
> _Photo shows: Rows of generic and name-brand medicine bottles on a pharmacy shelf._

Marisol's insurance doesn't cover the name-brand medication. Jennifer the pharmacist suggests an option, and Marisol also decides to call the insurance company herself.

- **Jennifer:** The name brand isn't covered. But try taking the generic. It's the same medicine, just cheaper. Most people do fine with it.
- **Marisol:** OK. I'll try taking it for a month and see.
- **Jennifer:** You can also try to get the name brand covered. Call the insurance line and ask for a prior authorization.
- **Marisol:** I'll try to call them tomorrow, but those calls are hard. You're on hold for an hour.

Notice the two different uses: try taking (try the generic, it's an experiment, easy to do) and try to call (try to reach the insurance, it's difficult, may not succeed).

try + -ing = experiment with something to see if it works. Low effort, worth trying.

try + to + verb = make an effort to do something. It may be difficult. You might not succeed.

**Exercise: Experiment or effort?**
- "Try taking a smaller dose if the side effects are bad." _(options: Experiment, try this and see if it helps / Effort, something difficult to accomplish)_
- "She tried to get the insurance to cover it, but they refused." _(options: Experiment, easy to try / Effort, she made an attempt but it was hard / didn't work)_

## 4. Remember to Take It / I Remember Taking It

> 🖼 **Scene:** Saturday morning, 7:00 AM. Marisol's phone alarm goes off.  
> _Photo shows: A phone screen showing a morning alarm reminder to take medication._

Marisol set a phone alarm so she doesn't forget her morning pill. Her husband Julio walks into the kitchen and asks if she already took it.

- **Julio:** Did you take your pill this morning?
- **Marisol:** Yes. I remember taking it at seven. I had it with my coffee.
- **Julio:** Good. And tomorrow, remember to take it before you leave. You have the early shift.
- **Marisol:** I know. The alarm is set. Don't worry. I always remember to take it now.

Two uses, two meanings. Remember taking it means she's looking back at something she already did. Remember to take it means she's looking forward at something she must not forget.

remember + to + verb = don't forget to do it (future / task). Looking forward.

remember + -ing = you recall doing it (past / memory). Looking back.

**Exercise: Future task or past memory?**
- "Remember to pick up your prescription on Thursday." _(options: Future task, don't forget to do this / Past memory, recalling something that happened)_
- "I remember taking the last pill on Sunday morning." _(options: Future task, a reminder for later / Past memory, recalling something already done)_

**Exercise: Choose the correct form**
- Don't forget, ___ your pill before the shift. (looking forward: task) _(options: remember taking / remember to take)_
- I ___ the form at the clinic. I signed it myself. (looking back: memory) _(options: remember filling out / remember to fill out)_

## 5. The Pairs Together

> 🖼 **Scene:** Marisol's kitchen, Sunday afternoon. Her neighbor Fatuma comes over.  
> _Photo shows: Two women talking at a kitchen table on a Sunday afternoon._

Marisol’s neighbor Fatuma has questions about her new prescription and remembering her pills. They read the instructions and decide to call the pharmacist.

- **Fatuma:** It says: 'Stop taking ibuprofen while on this medication.' What does that mean?
- **Marisol:** It says to stop taking ibuprofen while you’re on this medicine. Let’s call the pharmacist to check.
- **Fatuma:** Yes, please. I also keep forgetting my pills when I’m busy.
- **Marisol:** I used to forget too. Now I stop to take mine when my phone alarm goes off.
- **Marisol:** Try setting an alarm. It helps me remember to take them.
- **Fatuma:** I’ll do that. First, let’s call and check the instructions.

Quick reference:

stop + -ing = end an ongoing action

stop + to = pause to do something else

try + -ing = experiment, see if it works

try + to = make an effort (may be difficult)

remember + to = don't forget (future task)

remember + -ing = recall a past action (memory)

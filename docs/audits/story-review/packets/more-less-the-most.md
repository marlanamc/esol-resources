## 1. Which One Is Cheaper?

> 🖼 **Scene:** Market Basket, Chelsea Street. After a double shift, Rosa and Amara stop to compare prices.  
> _Photo shows: Grocery store produce aisle with colorful fruits and vegetables on display._

- **Rosa:** Beans are cheaper here than at the dollar store.
- **Amara:** Really? But the cooking oil is more expensive here.
- **Rosa:** True. The rice is better quality here, though.
- **Amara:** And the walk is shorter. My feet hurt.

Comparatives compare two things. Use them with than: Market Basket is cheaper than the dollar store.

Irregular comparatives

good better | bad worse | far farther / further

## 2. The Cheapest Plan I Could Find

> 🖼 **Scene:** Phone store on Meridian Street. Rosa compares three prepaid plans.  
> _Photo shows: Mobile phones displayed on a store counter with pricing labels visible._

- **Kevin:** I have three plans here. Which one do you want?
- **Rosa:** Which one is the cheapest?
- **Kevin:** Plan C. It is the least expensive at $35 a month.
- **Rosa:** And which one has the most data?
- **Kevin:** Plan B. But Plan C is the best value for the price.

Superlatives compare three or more things and name the one at the top (or bottom). Always use the before a superlative.

Irregular superlatives

good the best | bad the worst | far the farthest / furthest

**Exercise: Comparative or superlative?**
- Of the three plans, Plan C is ___. (cheap) _(options: cheaper (comparative) / the cheapest (superlative) / most cheap)_
- Plan B is ___ than Plan C for data. (expensive) _(options: the most expensive (superlative) / more expensive (comparative) / expensiver)_
- Which plan is ___? (good) _(options: gooder / better / the best)_

**Exercise: Error correction**
- "Plan C is most cheapest of all." _(options: Correct as is. / Plan C is the most cheapest of all. / Plan C is the cheapest of all. (short adjective: just add -est, no 'most'))_
- "This plan is the more convenient for my schedule." _(options: Correct as is. / This plan is the most convenient for my schedule. (superlative needs 'most', not 'more') / This plan is convenientest for my schedule.)_

**Exercise: Write the superlative**
- Plan A is ___ (expensive) of the three options.
- Rosa chose Plan C because it is ___ (good) deal for the price.

## 3. More Rooms, Less Noise

> 🖼 **Scene:** Kitchen table, East Boston. Fernanda and Diego compare apartment listings on their phones.  
> _Photo shows: Person sitting at a table looking at apartment listings on a laptop and phone._

- **Fernanda:** This one on Bremen Street has more rooms but fewer closets.
- **Diego:** The one on Maverick has less noise. It is not on the main street.
- **Fernanda:** But it costs more. $200 more a month.
- **Diego:** Before we decide, let’s ask about the landlord. I want fewer problems than we had last time.

Use more for both countable and uncountable things. Use less for uncountable things. Use fewer for countable things you can count one by one.

**Exercise: Less or fewer?**
- The Bremen Street apartment has ___ closets than the Maverick listing. _(options: less (uncountable) / fewer (countable. you can count closets))_
- The Maverick apartment has ___ noise from the street. _(options: less (uncountable. noise is not countable) / fewer (countable))_
- Diego wants ___ stress with the landlord this year. _(options: less (uncountable. stress is not countable) / fewer (countable))_
- The new building has ___ maintenance problems than the old one. _(options: less (uncountable) / fewer (countable. you can count problems))_

## 4. The Best Deal This Week

> 🖼 **Scene:** Flower stall near St. Mary's, East Boston. Saturday morning.  
> _Photo shows: Outdoor flower stall with marigolds and colorful bouquets for sale._

- **Rosa:** The marigolds here are more expensive than last year.
- **Vendor:** Yes, but these are the freshest on the street. Look at the color.
- **Rosa:** The shop on Sumner has the lowest price. But it is farther.
- **Vendor:** Buy here and save the bus fare. It is the better deal today.
- **Rosa:** All right. I’ll take two bundles, please.

Comparison language appears everywhere: store signs, apartment ads, phone plans, and conversations. The same rules apply: -er / more for two things, -est / the most for three or more.

Comparison language in real life

better than / worse than (comparing two things)

the best / the worst (of all options)

the most + adjective / the least + adjective

more + noun / less + uncountable / fewer + countable

**Exercise: Reading real comparisons**
- "The flowers here are the freshest on the street." _(options: The flowers are fresher than one other shop. / The flowers are fresher than all other shops on the street. / The flowers were fresh yesterday.)_
- "This apartment is the cheapest 2BR near the 111 bus." _(options: This apartment is cheaper than one other apartment. / This apartment costs less than all other 2BRs near the 111 bus. / This apartment was cheap last year.)_

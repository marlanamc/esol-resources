## 1. How Long Have You Worked Here?

> 🖼 **Scene:** East Boston. A hotel on Meridian Street. Thursday morning.  
> _Photo shows: A clean hotel hallway with a housekeeping cart parked outside a room._

- **Jennifer (HR):** Thanks for coming in, Rosa. So, how long have you worked in housekeeping?
- **Rosa:** I have worked in housekeeping for three years.
- **Jennifer (HR):** And how long have you lived in East Boston?
- **Rosa:** I have lived here since 2021.

How long have you...? asks about something that started in the past and is still true right now. You answer with for or since.

This is the same present perfect you have seen before. "How long" just focuses it on duration, on asking how much time has passed.

## 2. For vs. Since: Which One?

> 🖼 **Scene:** Hotel break room. Claudette and Maria eat lunch before the afternoon shift.  
> _Photo shows: Two hotel workers chatting at a small table during a break._

- **Claudette:** How long have you been at this hotel?
- **Maria:** I've been here since March. About four months.
- **Claudette:** I've been here for two years. Started right when they opened the new wing.
- **Maria:** Nice. How long have you had this shift?
- **Claudette:** Only since January. Before that I had nights.

for = a length of time (how many days, months, years) since = a starting point (a date, a month, a year, an event)

for six months, for two years, for a long time, for three weeks

since March, since 2021, since I moved here, since last summer

Common mistake: ✗ "I have worked here since two years." ✓ "I have worked here for two years." ✓ "I have worked here since 2022."

**Exercise: For or since?**
- I have cleaned rooms ___ six months. _(options: for / since)_
- She has worked the morning shift ___ January. _(options: for / since)_
- He has lived in East Boston ___ three years. _(options: for / since)_

**Exercise: Spot the error**
- Which sentence is NOT correct? _(options: She has worked here for two years. / She has worked here since two years. / She has worked here since 2022.)_

**Exercise: Fill in the blank**
- Claudette has had the day shift ___ January. (for or since?)
- Maria has been at this hotel ___ four months. (for or since?)

## 4. Still Going vs. Finished

> 🖼 **Scene:** HR office. Jennifer reviews two applications before the final interview.  
> _Photo shows: A hiring manager reviewing a resume with a job applicant across a small office desk._

- **Jennifer (HR):** The first candidate wrote, “I worked at the Marriott for two years.” She’s available to start Monday.
- **Jennifer (HR):** The second wrote, “I have worked at the Marriott for two years.” She would need to give notice.
- **Mark (Manager):** So the second candidate still works there. Let’s ask when she could start.

Present Perfect = started in the past and still true right now. Past Simple = finished. That job, that apartment, that situation is done.

**Exercise: Still true or finished?**

- Claudette's old apartment was in Chelsea, but she moved. She ___ there for one year. _(options: has lived (still there) / lived (she moved away))_

**Exercise: Spot the error**

**Exercise: Fill in the correct tense**

## 5. Putting It All Together

> 🖼 **Scene:** Hotel HR office. Rosa's final interview round. Tuesday, 2 PM.  
> _Photo shows: Two people in a professional meeting, one taking notes and one speaking._

Before the interview, Rosa's coworker Yemi coaches her in the break room.

- **Yemi:** They'll ask how long. Don't say 'I worked.' Say 'I have worked' so they know you're still going.
- **Rosa:** OK. How long have I worked in housekeeping? Three years. Since 2021.
- **Yemi:** Perfect. And how long in East Boston?
- **Rosa:** I have lived here for five years. I know this neighborhood.

At the interview with Jennifer:

- **Jennifer (HR):** How long have you worked in hotel housekeeping?
- **Rosa:** I have worked in housekeeping for three years, since 2021.
- **Jennifer (HR):** And have you ever supervised other cleaners?
- **Rosa:** I haven't been a supervisor, but I've trained two new workers at my current job.

The negative is simple: haven't or hasn't + past participle. "I haven't worked a night shift before, but I can." That's an honest answer that still sounds professional.

Women's History Month: In the break room, there's a flyer from the hotel workers union. One question on it reads: "How long have you worked without a raise?" Yemi reads it out loud and laughs. "Too long," she says.

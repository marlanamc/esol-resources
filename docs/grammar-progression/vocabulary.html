Everyday American English vocabulary

Local review edition · Checked against local sources October 18, 2026 · Not deployed

Teach words and phrases students can use in a real conversation: asking for help, changing an appointment, describing a problem, or explaining a choice. This is a practical teaching selection, not a measured frequency ranking.

Exactly six core words or phrases every week from Week 6 through Week 36, including required review sets in Weeks 15 and 30. Every set has Word Lab audio and practice. Weeks 1–5 remain as already taught; there are no optional vocabulary sets in Weeks 6–36.

Speaking first; recognition alongside it

Former terms remain in the vocabulary library to preserve existing review history; they are not additional assigned sets. Keep useful real-world terms such as prescription, dose, symptoms, gross pay, deductions, and benefits: students encounter them at pharmacies and at work. Teach a plain-English explanation and a useful phrase beside them.

Oxford labels ailment rather formal. For speaking practice, use a specific problem such as “I have a sore throat.” The U.S. Department of State’s American English resources also provide situational dialogues for natural everyday encounters.

A short fluency routine

  1. Hear and say a useful phrase, not only an isolated word.
  2. Personalize one sentence with a real or imagined detail.
  3. Ask a partner a question and listen to the answer.
  4. Ask a follow-up or say “Can you say that again?”
  5. Try again after feedback; award meaningful effort, with accuracy as an additional bonus.

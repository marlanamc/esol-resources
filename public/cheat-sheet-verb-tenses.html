Verb form key (same as Tense Formulas in the app): V1 = base form · V1-3rd = 3rd person -s · V1-ing = continuous -ing · V2 = past simple · V3 = past participle

Special Tense Uses

Present Continuous → Near Future

I am meeting them tomorrow at 3 pm.

Aff. subject + am/is/are + V1-ing
Neg. subject + am/is/are + not + V1-ing
Q. Am/Is/Are + subject + V1-ing?
Key Event is arranged or scheduled — needs a time: tonight, tomorrow, on Friday
vs. "I'll call you" = decision now  ·  "I'm calling Ana at 5" = already arranged
"Going to" → Future Plans & Predictions

I am going to call her tomorrow.

Aff. subject + am/is/are + going to + V1
Neg. subject + am/is/are + not + going to + V1
Q. Am/Is/Are + subject + going to + V1?
Key Pre-decided plans, OR predictions based on present evidence
vs. will = decision/promise made NOW  ·  going to = already decided
Present Simple → Future Timetable

The train leaves at 6 pm tonight.

Aff. subject + V1 / V1-3rd
Neg. subject + doesn't/don't + V1
Q. Does + subject + V1? / When does it V1?
Key Fixed timetables only — transport, shows, programs, class schedules
Not Not for personal plans → use present cont. or going to instead
Used To / Would / Past Habit

I used to walk / would walk to school.

Aff. subject + used to + V1
Neg. subject + didn't use to + V1
Also subject + would + V1  (actions only)
Freq. subject + used to / would + frequency marker + V1
always, usually, often, sometimes, every day, twice a week
Key used to = habits & states  ·  would = actions only (not states)
e.g. "I used to be shy" ✓  ·  "I would be shy" ✗
⚠ be used to + V-ing = accustomed to  ·  get used to + V-ing = become accustomed
Common 2-Verb Patterns (Context Controls the Tense)
Interruption

When I arrived, she was cooking.

Earlier Past

When I arrived at the station, the train had already left.

Future Clause

When I start my job, I will take the bus.

Rule 1 when + past simple often interrupts a background past continuous action
Rule 2 past perfect marks the earlier of two past actions
Rule 3 in future time clauses, use present after when/if, then will + V1 in the main clause

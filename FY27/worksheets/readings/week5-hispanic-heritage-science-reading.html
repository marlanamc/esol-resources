Reading: Science Stars

Name: ______________________

Hispanic Heritage Month is September 15 – October 15. Let’s meet two Hispanic Americans in science.

A Before you read
aerospace engineer – a person who designs airplanes and spacecraft surgeon – a doctor who operates on the body rover – a robot car that explores another planet crops – plants that farmers grow, like tomatoes broadcast – a TV or internet show that many people watch exhausting – very, very tiring transfer – move to a different school cure – medicine that stops a sickness

Talk: Both people in these stories came to the U.S. and learned English. What was hard for you when you started learning English? What helped you?

Diana Trujillo
Aerospace engineer, Mars mission leader

1Diana Trujillo was born in 1983 in Cali, Colombia. At age 17, she moved to the United States with only $300. She didn’t speak any English.

2Diana started English classes at Miami Dade College, a community college in Florida. Because she needed money for school, she worked full time while she studied. Her days were long and exhausting, but she didn’t give up.

3At college, Diana decided to become an aerospace engineer. She transferred to the University of Florida and then to the University of Maryland, where she finished her degree in 2007. While she was still a student, she was accepted into the NASA Academy. She was the first Hispanic immigrant woman in the program.

4In 2008, Diana started working at NASA’s Jet Propulsion Laboratory in California. Her team works on rovers that explore Mars. She helped lead missions for two rovers, Curiosity and Perseverance.

5When Perseverance landed on Mars in February 2021, Diana hosted NASA’s first live broadcast of a mission in Spanish. Families all over Latin America watched the landing in their own language. Today, Diana encourages young Latinas to study science.

Source: NASA, “Diana Trujillo: From Colombia to Mars.” Adapted for English learners.

Alfredo Quiñones-Hinojosa
Farmworker, student, brain surgeon

1Alfredo Quiñones-Hinojosa was born in 1968 near Mexicali, Mexico. He was the oldest of six children. His parents were farmworkers, and the family didn’t have much money.

2In 1987, when he was 19, Alfredo came to California to look for work. He spoke very little English. For months, he picked tomatoes and other crops in the hot sun. Even though the farm work was exhausting, he took English classes at night at San Joaquin Delta College, a community college.

3His teachers saw that he was a talented student, and they encouraged him to keep going. He transferred to the University of California, Berkeley. After that, he was accepted to Harvard Medical School, one of the best medical schools in the world. There, he decided to become a brain surgeon.

4Today, people call him “Dr. Q.” He works at the Mayo Clinic in Florida, where he operates on patients’ brains. He also studies brain cancer because he hopes to find a cure. He teaches young doctors, too.

5In 2011, he wrote a book about his life called Becoming Dr. Q. He went from the tomato fields to one of the most famous hospitals in the country.

Source: Mayo Clinic; KQED, “Meet the Brain Surgeon Who Once Picked Tomatoes.” Adapted for English learners.

E Subjects and verbs

7. work  ·  8. explores  ·  9. operates  ·  10. They

2 and 4 start with a time phrase, so the subject is not the first word. 5 is a long subject: the whole group is who watched. 7: Diana and her team = they, so no -s.

F Talk and write

Answers will vary. Check that each sentence has a subject and a verb, and that he / she / one person gets -s (works, studies).

How to use it: About 45–60 minutes. Go over the words in A together, then do the Talk question. It connects the stories to learners’ own English journeys.
Option – partner jigsaw: Partner A reads Diana, Partner B reads Alfredo. Each person tells their partner 3 facts (“She…” / “He…”), then they do B and D together. This gets a lot of he/she + -s talk.
Model E on the board with the example before learners start. If time is short, assign F for homework.
Background: Diana Trujillo is an aerospace engineer at NASA’s Jet Propulsion Laboratory and hosted #JuntosPerseveramos, NASA’s first Spanish-language broadcast of a mission event (Perseverance landing, Feb. 18, 2021). Dr. Alfredo Quiñones-Hinojosa is a neurosurgeon at Mayo Clinic in Florida; his memoir is Becoming Dr. Q.

Leading Station 2: SPEAK (B and V)

Packet pages 4–5

Many learners say b for v (or mix them). Spanish has one sound for both letters, so this is very normal. Goal: they hear the difference, then make it.

3 minLips or teeth? Show it big. B: press lips, pop. V: top teeth on bottom lip, buzz. Have everyone look at their phone camera and say boat / vote.
5 minA. Listen and circle. Read the words below. Say each word twice, naturally. Don’t over-exaggerate the first time. Check answers together.
4 minB. Partner game. Walk around. If a partner points to the wrong word, model it once, slowly.
3 minC. Which sentence? Read the sentence below for each number.
5 minD. Read aloud. One sentence each, around the table. Fix only the b/v sounds.
5 minE. Talk. Everyone answers at least one. Push for a full sentence and one follow-up.

A. Say these words

1vote5bet
2best6vase
3van7curb
4very8vent

Time left? Do it again with the other word in each pair.

C. Say these sentences

  1. b. They counted the votes.
  2. a. This is my best one.
  3. a. Take my dog to the vet.
  4. b. Park near the curve.

When they say it wrong

  • Point to your mouth: lips (B) or teeth (V). Let them try again.
  • Fix one word, then move on. Keep it light.
  • In Part E, repeat it back correctly. Don’t stop them.
Learner: “I buy bejetables every week.”
You: “Oh, you buy vegetables every week? Which ones?”

Adjust the level

  • Beginner: Just repeat after you, word by word.
  • Stronger: They say a word for the group to circle.

Station 3: READ, Election Night (pages 6–7)

A: 1 Answers vary: it’s her first time; she doesn’t know the steps; she is a new citizen. · 2 2 fills in circles, 1 name and address, 4 sticker, 3 ballot in machine · 3 Yes: he called his city councilor’s office and the city fixed the streetlight. · 4 a city · b state · c Congress · ★5 Not sure. He will vote only if he passes his test. · ★6 No. She says if (“If my candidate wins… If my candidate loses…”). Level 3 can stop at Q4. Q5–6 connect the story to the grammar: first conditional = possible, not certain.

B, Zero (any 3): If you vote by mail, you don’t wait in line. / If a streetlight breaks, you contact the city. / If you have a problem on your street, you contact your city or town. / If you have a question about a state law, you contact your state representative. / If you have a question about a national law, you contact your members of Congress. (“If you are a U.S. citizen… you can vote” is also always true. Accept it.)

B, First (any 3): If I leave work at 5, I will vote before class. / If I pass my test, I will vote in the next election. / If you need help with the questions, I will study with you. / If my candidate wins, I will celebrate… / If my candidate loses, I will be sad.

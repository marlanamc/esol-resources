Volunteer Guide: TALK

Week 3, Tuesday

Thank you! Your job is to get every learner talking. Talking matters more than perfect English today.

Please do

  • Wait 5 seconds after you ask. Silence means thinking.
  • Point to the help sentences on the Talk Card if someone is stuck.
  • Repeat it back correctly. Don't stop them to correct.
    Learner: “Me family relocate 2022.” → You: “Oh, my family relocated in 2022? Why?”
  • Ask “Why?” and “Tell me more.”
  • Share a little about you. Learners like to ask you questions too.
  • Make sure everyone talks. Invite quiet learners by name.

Please don't

  • Correct in the middle of a sentence or finish their sentences.
  • Push for details. Some journeys are painful. “Pass” is always OK. Move on with a smile.

Adjust the level

  • Beginner: Stay on questions 1–3. Give choices: “Happy or tired?”
  • Stronger: Ask them to turn the Talk Card over. Use the follow-up questions.
Last 2 minutes: Write a Great Sentence slip for each learner. Copy one good thing they said. You can add one small “Try this” tip.

Teacher Table: FIX

Station 2 · teacher copy

Pronouns + articles. This previews Thursday's “Find the errors” warm-up.

Routine (about 12 min)

  • I do (2 min): Think aloud on #1. “Whose family? Mine. Before a noun, I use my.”
  • We do (5 min): #2–#5 on mini whiteboards. Everyone shows the answer at the same time.
  • You do (5 min): Each learner finds and fixes one sentence in their own writing or warm-up.

Practice sentences

  • 1. Me family relocated here in 2022. → My
  • 2. Him is my teacher. → He
  • 3. I came to a United States five years ago. → the
  • 4. She has a apartment in Chelsea. → an
  • 5. My children motivate I. → me
  • 6. Her works in a hospital. → She
  • 7. An job helps my family. → A
  • 8. I want to achieve a my goal. → (delete a)

Write the sentences without the underlines and answers. #6–#8 are for fast groups.

Watch for: which learners still mix up he/she/her? Use Thursday's warm-up to check again.

Pronouns: where is the word?

Before the verbAfter the verbBefore a noun

Articles: a, an, or the?

  • a + consonant sound → a job, a city, a new home
  • an + vowel sound (a, e, i, o, u) → an immigrant, an apartment, an English class
  • the = we know which one → the teacher, the bus to school
  • the + some country names → the United States
  • No article with my / his / her → a my goal → my goal

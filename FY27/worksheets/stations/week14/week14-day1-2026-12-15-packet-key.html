EAST SIDE WEEKLYDecember 14, 2026 · Practice article: the newspaper, people, and prices are made up

Your Grocery Bill: What Costs More This Year?

Words to know
grocery bill = what you pay for food
shopper = a person who shops
a dozen = 12
rise = go up
economist = an expert on money and the economy
paycheck = the money you get for your work

Shoppers in East Side are talking about prices. “My grocery bill is bigger than last year,” says Maria Lopes, a mother of three. “I buy the same food, but I spend more.”

The East Side Weekly checked prices at three local stores in December 2025 and December 2026. Some foods are more expensive now. Coffee had the biggest change. A pound of coffee costs $1.50 more than last year.

Eggs are also more expensive. A dozen eggs costs $4.20 now. Last year, it was $3.50. But not everything costs more. Milk is a little cheaper than last year, and chicken is the same price.

Why do prices change? When prices rise for a long time, economists call it inflation. Weather, gas prices, and wages can all change food prices. “When coffee farms have a bad year, there is less coffee, so it is more expensive,” says a local store owner.

Wages matter too. If prices go up faster than wages, families can buy less with their paychecks. Maria has a plan. “I compare unit prices, and I buy rice and beans in bulk. It’s the cheapest way to feed my family.”

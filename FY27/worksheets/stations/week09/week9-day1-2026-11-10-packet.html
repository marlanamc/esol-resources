STATION 3: READSaturday at the Food Pantry · with your group
Page 6
Words to know
food pantry = a place that gives free food
shift = your work time, like 9 to 12
sign in = write your name when you arrive
gloves = you wear them on your hands
expired = too old; the date is past
heavy = hard to lift

Every Saturday morning, the Eastside Community Food Pantry gives free food to families. Nobody gets paid. Volunteers do all the work. They sort cans, pack bags, and help families carry food to their cars.

Mei moved to Boston last year. She wants to practice her English and meet new people, so she signed up for a Saturday shift online. The website says, “Volunteers must be 16 or older. Kids 12 to 15 can volunteer with a parent.” Mei’s son, Leo, is 13, so he can come with her.

On Saturday at 8:45, Mei and Leo walk into the church basement. A woman with a clipboard smiles. “Hi! I’m Grace, the volunteer leader. First, you must sign in. Then you should wash your hands and put on gloves.”

Grace takes them to a long table with boxes of cans. “Today you can sort the cans,” she says. “Check the date on every can. If a can is expired, put it in the red box. You shouldn’t give old food to families.” Leo finds a can of soup from 2024. “Mom, this one is expired!” he says.

At 10:00, the doors open and families come in. Some families bring their own bags. Others take bags from the pantry. A man asks Mei a question in Spanish. Mei can’t speak Spanish, but another volunteer, Jorge, can. He helps the man find rice and beans.

Some boxes are very heavy. Leo tries to pick one up. “Leo, you shouldn’t lift that box alone,” Grace says. “Two people must carry the big boxes.” Jorge and Leo carry it together.

At 12:00, the shift is over. Mei is tired but happy. “Can we come back next week?” Leo asks. “Of course,” Grace says. “You can sign up online for any Saturday. And you should bring a water bottle next time. It gets hot in here!”

On the way home, Mei thinks about her neighbors. Many of them work on Saturdays. “They can’t volunteer on Saturdays,” she thinks, “but they can donate food. Everyone can help in some way.”

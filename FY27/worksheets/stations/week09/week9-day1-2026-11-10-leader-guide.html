Leading Station 2: SPEAK (can / can’t)

Packet pages 4–5

In a sentence, can is weak: I c’n SWIM /kən/. Can’t is strong: I CAN’T swim /kænt/, and the t is often silent. Learners listen for the t and miss it. Teach them to listen for the beat. In short answers both are strong: Yes, I CAN. / No, I CAN’T.

3 minThe beat. Clap it: I can SWIM (da da DUM) vs. I CAN’T swim (da DUM da). Have everyone clap and say both.
5 minA. Listen and circle. Read each sentence below once, at normal speed. Don’t say the t hard. That’s the point. Then check.
4 minB. Thumbs. Partners read Part A sentences. Listen in. If thumbs are wrong, ask the reader to say it again with the beat.
5 minC. Who can do it? At the table only. Push for loud short answers: Yes, I CAN.
4 minD. Read aloud. One sentence each. Fix only can / can’t.
4 minE + F. Talk, then each person reads you their two sentences (F).

A. Read these sentences

1I can’t drive.
2She can come on Saturday.
3We can help out on Sunday.
4He can’t lift heavy boxes.
5They can’t speak Spanish.
6You can park here.
7My son can’t swim.
8I can volunteer this week.

Time left? Read them again and switch can ↔ can’t.

When they say it wrong

  • Most common: a strong CAN that sounds like can’t. Clap the beat with them.
  • Fix one thing, then move on. Keep it light.
  • In Part E, repeat it back correctly. Don’t stop them.
Learner: “I CAN cook.” (sounds like can’t)
You: “Oh, you can COOK? What do you cook?”

Adjust the level

  • Beginner: Part A with 4 sentences, then repeat after you.
  • Stronger: They read Part A for the group to circle.

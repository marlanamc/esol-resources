Massachusetts PROTECT Act: What’s Changing

Staff Quick Info · Oct. 2026

A new state law limits when people can be arrested for immigration reasons in some places, and adds protections for families and people in detention. Some parts are already in effect. More start Nov. 3 (page 2).

In effect now

Schools + health careSince Sept. 1
  • No immigration arrests at public + charter schools or licensed child care without a judge’s warrant
  • Same rule in areas hospitals + clinics mark as private, like exam rooms (since Oct. 4)
  • Places of worship are not covered. Lobbies + waiting rooms only if marked private.
Family emergency planningSince Aug. 5
  • Caregiver affidavit: no court, but must be notarized and signed in front of 2 adult witnesses (not the caregiver). Caregiver must live with the child. Covers school + medical decisions for up to 2 years; parent keeps all rights
  • Standby guardian: named on court form MPC 142. Starts if the parent is detained or deported. Guardian must file with Probate and Family Court within 30 days, unless a judge already confirmed it
  • Plan before a crisis. MIRA family guide on page 3.
Police + courtsSince Sept. 4
  • Police, sheriffs, and court staff generally can’t ask about or record immigration status (some exceptions)
  • Generally can’t hold someone just because ICE asks, or share private info with ICE
  • This limits local help to ICE. It does not stop ICE itself.
ICE inspection at workSince Aug. 5
  • If ICE says it will check workers’ I-9 papers (proof of work permission), the employer generally must tell all employees in writing within 48 hours
  • Workers get a warning. ICE can still check the papers.
Urgent? Detention, transfer, or a court date coming up: get the student to a lawyer right away. Lawyer directory on page 3.

PROTECT Act: Starting Nov. 3

Staff Quick Info · Oct. 2026
Courthouse arrestsStarts Nov. 3
  • No ICE arrests anywhere on court property (the “curtilage”): steps, sidewalks, lawns, plazas, and court parking lots, without a judge’s warrant or order
  • Before any arrest, ICE must show a judge at that courthouse the warrant or order. The judge reviews it
  • No immigration arrests inside a courtroom
  • Court is not fully safe. Students with a court date should talk to a lawyer first.
Detention + transfersStarts Nov. 3
  • State and county jails holding people for ICE must explain rights in the person’s language and allow free video calls with lawyers
  • If someone is moved, the jail must try to tell their lawyer or family contact within 6 hours, including where they went
  • Each needs a public phone number to check who is held there
  • Not every place ICE holds people is covered.
U + T visa formsStarts Nov. 3
  • For some crime or trafficking survivors. Needs a signed form from police or another agency
  • Agencies generally must answer within 45 days, or 14 business days if urgent
  • The signed form is not a visa. Wage theft or housing problems alone usually don’t qualify.
Most of these rules need a judge’s warrant. ICE usually doesn’t have one.
ICE warrant: does NOT count
  • Says “Department of Homeland Security” at the top (Form I-200 or I-205)
  • Signed by an immigration officer
Judge’s warrant: counts
  • Names a court, like “Trial Court of Massachusetts” or “U.S. District Court”
  • Signed by a judge or magistrate

Check the signature line. Real samples on page 4. Staff can ask to see the paper. Don’t physically block anyone. Call a lawyer or LUCE.
